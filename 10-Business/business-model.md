# Business model

**What this is:** how Parakeet Communities makes money, who it serves, how a job moves from enquiry to cash, and where the machine currently grinds. The page a new General Manager would want on day one.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

### What the business is

- Parakeet Communities is a vertically integrated owner and operator of manufactured housing communities and RV parks. It acquires, operates, improves and ultimately sells communities, handling everything in between with its own people.
- **The product is the community.** In the owner's words: "we sell a home, a lifestyle, a community, a safe place to live and more. Rent it more than sell it. We rent the land but sell the homes on it. We don't want the homes and the additional overhead and expenses that go along with them."
- So the model has two halves that pull in opposite directions on purpose: **the land is rented and held**, and **the homes on it are sold and shed**. Lot rent is the durable revenue; home sales are the mechanism that fills lots and moves park-owned homes off the books. See [[50-Products-Sales/the-community]] and [[50-Products-Sales/home-sales]].
- Mission, from the Employee Handbook: "To bring outstanding, affordable living to our current and future residents without sacrificing quality and efficiency."
- Stated pricing philosophy, from the March 2026 executive strategy deck: "As a philosophy we want to maximize lot rent and minimize home price. This transfers value to our NOI and land value."

### Scale and footprint

- **67 active communities.** 5,293 MH lots, 950 RV sites, 136 other units, 6,379 core units total. 784 park-owned homes. 934.3 acres. Aggregate purchase basis $487,974,500. All as of the Property Data Sheet, occupancy vintage December 2025.
- Weighted occupancy **82.5%** at December 2025, across the 62 properties with reliable data. Five rows are unreliable and are excluded — see [[60-Communities/portfolio]].
- **Live operating states: Florida (47 properties), South Carolina (7), Massachusetts (5), Georgia (5), North Carolina (1), Alabama (1).** One Michigan property, Lamberton Lake, is managed and being sold. Cambridge MHC in West Virginia has been sold and is archived.
- Eight ownership vehicles: JREFI (22 properties), JREFII (22), Origins (10), Single Asset (4), Baker (3), Kippy (3), Harpreet (2), Fund II (1). The same properties carry different portfolio labels in different workbooks, which is a known data problem.
- **Acquisition history is front-loaded and recent: 25 of the 67 were acquired in 2025**, 12 in 2022, 15 in 2021, 2 in 2026. More than a third of the portfolio is still being stabilised rather than maintained.
- ~125 employees total, ~42 corporate including offshore support. Two offices: [[10-Business/locations/maryland-office]] is HQ, [[10-Business/locations/miami-office]] is the capital side. The field is the third location in practice.

### Who buys

- **Two customer bases.** Residents, split between homeowners who own the home and rent the lot, and renters who rent a company-owned home. And capital partners: Jadian as the forward-looking equity partner, Walton Street as the largest debt relationship, plus lenders, brokers and the Bedrock Fund II LP base now in wind-down. See [[30-Customers/customers]].
- Why residents choose Parakeet, in the owner's framing: **the total payment math.** Solve for the resident's total monthly payment against a two-bedroom rental in that market, price the home low and hold the lot rent, and the resident ends up owning something for less than renting. Competition is the large institutional MHC operators — see [[10-Business/competitors]].

### How the work moves

- Five operating departments, and the March deck is explicit that cross-functioning between them is the critical thing: **Asset Management plus Accounting & Finance are the central hub.** See [[10-Business/departments/property-operations]] and its siblings.
- Five cross-departmental workflows: Home Sale, Home Demo, Vacant Lot In-fill, Capex Project, Home Rehab. Property Operations initiates, Sales & Marketing and Construction execute, Accounting & Finance reviews invoices start to finish, Asset Management tracks to budget and holds departments accountable.
- One job end to end: lead in and logged immediately, application and AmRent screening, offer via PSA with Director of Sales approval inside 24 hours, deal-type routing to cash, financed or lease-with-option, closing package, funds confirmed, keys handed, move-in request the same day, and Asset Management moves Prospect to Resident only after payment is confirmed and the floorplan loan is paid off. Full detail at [[50-Products-Sales/pipeline]].
- The infill engine: a vacant or turned lot is identified, a home is ordered from a manufacturer, delivered, set, punched, and listed — then sold, converting a vacant lot into an occupied one with a homeowner paying lot rent. 2026 plan: order ~230 homes, list ~400, sell ~400 at a maximum 30% loss.
- **Rent Manager is the system of record for everything** and every process routes through it. See [[10-Business/tools-and-systems]].

### The week and the month

- Weekly: Monday 8:30am founders call, Tuesday 2 to 5pm one-on-ones, Friday 2pm Executive EOS call. No meetings before 9am. No meetings without prior documentation or a stated reason.
- Monthly, and this is the real rhythm of the business: 1st AR pulled and the reminder sequence starts, 6th demand notices produced and served, 8th to 25th daily AR review, 25th rent roll out to community managers, 27th month-end JotForm due at noon, 29th Director of Operations signs off, last week of the month intake packets to the attorney. Full detail at [[80-Finance/money-flow]].
- Seasonal: Florida is a snowbird market. August is dead and the season drives the year, which is why the sales ramp targets October.

### How money comes in

- Lot rent monthly, billed in Rent Manager with resident auto-pay through Tenant Web Access. Utility reimbursements billed pro rata on total lot basis regardless of whether a lot is occupied, so reimbursement income rises with occupancy. Home sale proceeds in cash, through a consumer lender, or as monthly lease-option payments. RV transient and seasonal bookings. Ancillary income from boat slips, the Geiger Key Fish Camp restaurant and fees.
- All vendor invoices at NET 30 through Avid Exchange. Field spend on Ramp cards with a GL code required in the memo.

### What is written down, and what is not

- 109 processes are tracked across a documented nine-band numbering scheme. **Exactly one is approved.** Bands 400 Asset Management, 500 Acquisitions, 600 Systems and 700 HR are empty. See [[70-Operations/sops/sop-library]].
- Nineteen conflicts are documented and open, several of them statutory. Massachusetts and North Carolina are live operating states with no compliance SOPs. No RV compliance process exists at all.
- Decisions are recorded in email, ClickUp, decks, transcripts and often nowhere. The owner names this as the problem. [[00-Meta/decisions-log]] is the fix.
