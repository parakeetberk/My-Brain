# Competitors

**What this is:** who residents and sellers compare Parakeet to, what those competitors do well, and why customers choose Parakeet instead.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

- **Who they are up against: the large institutional MHC operators.** The owner named this category on 2026-08-30 — the national consolidators and the regional aggregators running the same playbook at greater scale. Individual competitor names are not yet recorded; that is a gap on this page.
- **What that tier does well:** cheaper cost of capital, so they can pay more for the same asset and wait longer for the return. Established brands with sellers and brokers. Mature process and reporting infrastructure. Purchasing scale on homes, materials and services. Sales organisations that already exist rather than being built.
- On the acquisition side, the practical competitive fact is the one in the numbers: 2025 acquisition volume came in at $171.5M against a $273.8M budget, with a pause in the back third of the year. Deal profile across the portfolio is overwhelmingly off-market, with mom-and-pop sellers dominant — 60 of 67 properties were off-market. **Parakeet competes on sourcing off-market from mom-and-pop owners rather than winning marketed processes.**

### Why residents pick Parakeet — the owner's answer

- **The total payment math.** Solve for the resident's total monthly payment against a comparable two-bedroom rental in that market, then price the home low enough that owning beats renting. The model: a $65,000 home plus $35,000 of infill cost financed at 10% over 20 years with 5% down, benchmarked against a two-bedroom market rent of $1,448 less a 10% discount, so $1,303.20. At full cost the resident's total payment is $1,569.89, about 20% above the benchmark, and only drops below it at roughly a 20 to 30% discount. Hence the policy: start at a 25% discount. "Discount = velocity."
- That is the argument, and it is worth being honest about what it means: **the competitive edge is priced in, not free.** Winning the resident costs 25 to 30% of the home's cost, recovered through lot rent and land value over time rather than at the point of sale.

### The other competitive frame

- Residents are not only choosing between communities. They are choosing between a Parakeet home and an apartment, or between owning and continuing to rent. The website's positioning speaks to that directly — "Live Simply. Live Well. Live Parakeet." and affordable living without sacrificing quality.
- A distinct competitive pressure inside the communities: **"landlords"** — third parties who own homes on Parakeet lots and rent them out. One tour recording notes "over 10 landlords in the park." They compete for the same resident while contributing none of the standards, which is part of why converting renters to owners matters.
