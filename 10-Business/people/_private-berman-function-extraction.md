That plan was written without knowledge of the offboarding. **As it stands it deepens dependency on someone who is leaving**, and its critical path runs through him.

**This is the most important sequencing decision on the table**, because the two options point in opposite directions:

**Option A — extract the knowledge first, then exit.** Berman signs the entity list and rules on eliminations before he goes. Cleanest possible handover; the successor inherits a settled foundation rather than an argument. **Cost**: the timeline is his, and the 7/27 exchange suggests it will not be fast.

The wind-down and the audit both then run on an unsettled base.

**Recommendation: a narrow version of A.** Do not attempt the full eight-year reconciliation before he leaves. Extract only what is irreplaceable and cannot be reconstructed from records:

- The **entity list with EINs**, signed
- A **written ruling on the elimination ledger** — it governs $2.7M of the $5.4M and is a methodology question, not a data question

- **What actually came out of the 7/29 2:00 PM call**
- Status of **D-4, EKEET, $1,147,531** — currently assigned to "whoever prepared the 2023 cleanup," i.e. nobody

Those five items are the difference between a successor inheriting a foundation and inheriting an archaeology project. Everything else in the AR/AP plan can be rebuilt by Moe from records.
