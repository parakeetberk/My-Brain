Where the program stands against the board goals

Two 2026 board goals run through this program, both from the March deck.

This is the one part of the program that is working, and nobody is reporting it. 9.6 completions a month against four months left puts the year around 115. On the second goal — turn any vacated home in under 60 days — 57 of 67 measurable turns came in at or under 60 days, median 31. The failure is entirely in the tail: 18 open units past 60 days, 15 past 90, and 40 more with no start date at all so they are not on the clock.
John Clark’s Q1 rock is two units from done, five months late. “Complete all rehabs backlogged as of 3/31” has SOUTH-86 (Southgate, 274 days, High Pace) and BRUN-203 (Brunswick Estates, 250 days, Primary Home Repair) left. Everything else from that backlog has closed. The rock reads as failed and is two units from complete — worth saying out loud at the call.

WIP, invoices, and the million-dollar question

There are seven steps between an approved scope and a rehab dollar landing on the balance sheet. This company can see three of them. Invoice received, invoice approved and paid, and unit placed in service are captured nowhere.

Origins and Kippy have no vendor tracker coverage at all — $86,290 of GL spend against zero vendor records, plus $697,334 of modelled remaining. The Wednesday call reads from that tracker, so it has never seen either fund. Meanwhile JREFII’s GL sits $195,142 above its tracker figure while JREFI reconciles to within 1%, so the divergence is fund-specific rather than systemic — which makes it findable. JREFII is also the fund carrying the Responsible Contractor Policy question (KP-0014, audit due 2026-09-30).

The closing balance is the only line that can be approximated today, and even that assumes every completed unit has already rolled out — which is exactly what is in doubt. Two rules once the table exists: a unit enters WIP when the first invoice is coded to it, not when the scope is approved, and it leaves when it is available to rent or sell, not when the last invoice is paid. Those are different dates and the gap between them is real depreciation.

The single biggest finding: 37 rows came in through a different door

Four things looked like four data problems. They are one cohort, and the partition is perfect. 37 rows carry a unit-code prefix in the community field instead of a community name — and those same 37 rows are, exactly, every defective row in the file.

So this is not data quality drifting. It is a second intake path that was never onboarded. The cohort is 16 internal Parakeet Ops jobs and 16 unassigned units — work the field did itself, or work nobody was given — carrying $386,175 of modelled scope and $19,374 of booked spend. The internal track has no cost discipline at all, which is precisely the work that never gets invoiced and therefore never gets questioned.

Every by-community rollup double-counts as a result: TO and The Oaks are the same place, and so are PARK/Parkwood, RW/Riverwalk, FOX/Fox Creek, SILVER/Silver Breeze, BRUN/Brunswick Estates, BOVIIPO/Bay Oaks Village II. Codes are resolved on this page where they can be; NGMH, LS and PPH cannot be resolved from anything on file and are shown as unresolved rather than guessed.

One fix, not four: an intake standard requiring a community from the master list, a tier, a WIP code, and a vendor record even when the vendor is us. This is KP-0037 surfacing in a third system — the master community list is the shared dependency, and the FastField cascades and QuickBase relationship fields key off the same list.
