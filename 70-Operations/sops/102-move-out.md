# 102 — Move out

**What this is:** a voluntary move-out of a **resident-owned** home — notice, exterior lot inspection, Regional approval, and the handoff to the buyer's residency application. Eviction move-outs are not covered here.

**Status:** in review (Tier 1 Critical). Target 2026-10-31. Read 2026-08-30.

## The facts

- **Owner:** Natalie Magurne, Director of Operations. **Executes:** resident (seller), Community/District Manager on-site, Regional Manager (approval authority), Home Office / Asset Management (Rent Manager execution). **Trigger:** the resident submits a written 30-Day Notice to Vacate. **Ends when:** Home Office moves the current resident out and the new resident in, in Rent Manager.
- Seven steps: written notice → on-site manager forwards to Home Office → Home Office updates Rent Manager and issues the move-out report → exterior inspection → Regional approval or decline → resident notified of the buyer process → Rent Manager transition once the buyer is approved.
- **The notice must be written. A verbal notice is not valid and does not start the 30-day period.** Nothing is forwarded and nothing is entered in Rent Manager until a written notice arrives. The period begins on the date the on-site manager receives it.
- **The key rule, stated three times in the document:** the current resident remains financially liable for all lot rent and charges **until the prospective buyer applies for residency AND is approved** — even if the resident has physically vacated, the sale has closed, the buyer has moved belongings in, or the 30 days have elapsed. Liability ends only when Home Office processes the transition in Rent Manager.
- The inspection is **exterior only** — lot condition, landscaping, exterior home condition, and any damage or outstanding issue that must be cleared before the lot can be approved for the next resident.
- **The Regional Manager, not the on-site manager, approves** both the lot condition and the resident's account. Not approved → the resident is told the specific issues, corrects them, and the on-site manager re-inspects; the loop repeats until the RM approves.
- **There is no deadline on correcting failed inspection items** — the SOP says so explicitly ("no automatic deadline, but the clock is running on the resident's financial obligations").
- The buyer must submit a formal residency application and be approved before taking occupancy. The manager must explain this, and the ongoing liability, clearly and early.
- **Systems used:** Rent Manager (move-out date, move-out report, resident transition, unit status, recurring charges).
- **Known problems:**
  - **Scope is far narrower than every index says it is.** The wiki index describes 102 as "Move-out, condition documentation, **utility transfers**." The SOP covers none of that: no utility transfer step, no security deposit clock, no interior inspection, and **no park-owned-home or rental move-out path at all** — only resident-owned, non-eviction.
  - **The security deposit clock is missing from the one process that starts it.** The 800-series deposit SOPs set hard statutory clocks running from termination of tenancy — FL 15 days to refund / 30 days to claim (806), GA inspect within 3 business days and return within 30 (814), SC 30 days (824) — and none of it appears in 102. The video schedule even lists "Rent Manager move-out entry + **deposit clock start**" as a 102 video topic.
  - **The photo standard the video schedule assumes does not exist in the SOP.** Track A week of Aug 24 2026: "Move-out inspection with photos… Show the condition-report photo standard." 102 requires no photos.
