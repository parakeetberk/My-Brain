# 122 — Safety & PPE

**What this is:** Parakeet's internal PPE program for field staff, aligned to OSHA — what to wear for which hazard, weekly inspection, heat stress management, and replacement.

**Status:** in review. Initial document created Aug 1 2026. Not on the tracker at all. Read 2026-08-30.

## The facts

- **Owner:** Regional Manager (process owner). **Executes:** Maintenance Technician (selects, wears, inspects, stores PPE), Maintenance Manager (confirms selection on non-routine/high-risk tasks, reviews checklists, monthly inventory), Community Manager (informal spot-checks, documents non-compliance), Regional Manager (oversight, approves replacement orders), Director of Operations (escalation for systemic non-compliance). **Trigger:** any time field staff begin on-site work involving a recognised hazard. **Ends when:** the task is complete and all PPE has been removed, inspected post-use and stored.
- **Scope excludes contractors and vendors** — they are responsible for their own PPE compliance.
- Five timelines: PPE selection → weekly inspection & readiness → task-based protocols → heat stress management → maintenance, storage & replacement.
- **PPE by hazard** — eye protection to ANSI Z87.1 (mowing, power tools, cutting pipe, grinding, chemicals, pressure washing, demolition); four glove classes (nitrile, cut-resistant, chemical-resistant, **voltage-rated insulating for breaker changes**); ASTM-rated safety-toe boots for **all** field work, waterproof for water/sewer, storm cleanup and pressure washing; hard hat for tree work, excavation, equipment operation, roofing and overhead hazards; hearing protection around lawn equipment, chainsaws, generators, compressors; and three respirator tiers — N95/dust mask, half-face (mold remediation, chemicals, painting), full-face (severe mold, hazardous chemical exposure).
- **PPE by task** — plumbing: safety glasses, nitrile gloves, waterproof boots (add face shield, coveralls, respirator if sewage present). **Electrical: breaker changes only** — safety glasses and voltage-rated gloves; **all major electrical work goes to a licensed vendor.** Groundskeeping: glasses, hearing protection, hi-vis vest, work gloves, safety boots. Demolition and unit turns: glasses, gloves, steel-toe boots (add respirator, coveralls, hard hat).
- **The three most common compliance gaps, named in the SOP:** safety glasses during mowing, long pants in the field, hearing protection around lawn equipment.
- **Weekly PPE Inspection Checklist at the start of each work week**, submitted via FastField Forms. Damaged, worn or defective PPE is removed and **must not be used until replaced.** The Maintenance Manager ensures replacement PPE is on site before the next shift.
- **Heat stress: check the heat index before outdoor work on any day forecast at or above 90°F.** Cooling towels, water stations, electrolyte packets and shaded rest areas must be in place **at the start of the shift — not sourced mid-day.** Technicians monitor themselves and each other for heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, cramps or nausea, report symptoms immediately and stop work.
- **Heat stroke is a medical emergency — confusion, loss of consciousness, hot dry skin, or body temperature above 103°F. Call 911 immediately, do not wait; notify the Regional Manager after calling.**
- Any injury or medical emergency from heat illness **triggers SOP 108 — Incident Report.**
- Replacement flow: Technician → Maintenance Manager → Regional Manager for approval and ordering. Maintenance Manager does a **full PPE inventory check monthly.**
- **New hires review this SOP during onboarding and sign an acknowledgment placed in their maintenance binder**, administered and filed by the Maintenance Manager. The SOP is reviewed annually or after any significant PPE incident or regulatory change.
- Non-compliance is documented by the Community Manager and reported to the Regional Manager; disciplinary action follows the standard performance improvement process.
- **Systems used:** FastField Forms (Weekly PPE Inspection Checklist), SharePoint > Training SOPs.
- **Known problems:**

  - **122 is not on the SOP Master Tracker.** There is no row for PPE, safety, or OSHA anywhere in v3 — so this SOP has no scoreboard entry, no tier, no target date and no reviewer.
  - **122 is not published in the wiki.** No `122 Safety & PPE` folder exists; the SOP, its flowchart and its weekly checklist sit only in the drafting room. The SOP's own header says "SOP Location: SharePoint > Training SOPs" — a third location, outside this library.
  - **It escalates into a process the tracker says does not exist.** SOP 108 (Incident Report) is named as the downstream trigger; the tracker lists Incident Reporting as a Tier 1 gap with nothing started.
  - Timeline 3 ends with an orphaned fragment — a bare line reading "clear." — indicating a truncated instruction.
  - Roles are duplicated with no reconciliation: **Maintenance Manager** enforces daily compliance while the **Community Manager** spot-checks and the **Regional Manager** owns the program; nothing says who is accountable when a technician works without PPE.
  - The disciplinary path points to "the standard performance improvement process," which per SOP 704 cannot be issued by a Regional Manager without HR and Director of Operations approval — the cross-reference is not made.
  - No LOTO (lockout/tagout) procedure exists, though `123-Parakeet_Maintenance_Tools draft.xlsx` requires a LOTO kit and cites "SOP 108 / OSHA 1910.147" — a citation to the incident-reporting SOP, which contains no LOTO content.
  - The SOP is reviewed annually with no first review date set; Effective Date is not stated.
