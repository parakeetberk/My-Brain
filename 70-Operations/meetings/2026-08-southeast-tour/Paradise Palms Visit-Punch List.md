# 08-22 Field Review: Construction Quality Control Failures in Stair Orientation and Concrete Work

[image]

## Core Synopsis

The field review identified significant quality control failures and a lack of standardized execution across construction crews, most notably in concrete work and stair installation. Inconsistent workmanship is creating rework, impacting user experience, and introducing unnecessary project risk. The most glaring issue is the orientation of stairs on several homes, which conflicts with door swings and logical access patterns, suggesting either gross negligence or a breakdown in site supervision between two distinct crews. While one crew's work is consistently correct, the other's is subpar, leading to avoidable fixes like re-skirting stairs or potentially re-hanging doors. This is not a materials problem; it's a process and accountability problem. **We are paying for the same job twice**—once for the initial flawed execution and again for the required remediation.

## Site Inspection: Quality Control Breakdown

### Execution Inconsistency Between Crews
An on-site inspection reveals a stark "night and day" difference in the quality and methodology of work, strongly indicating the deployment of two separate crews with different standards.

- **Correct Execution**: A significant portion of the concrete work within the park is described as "done correctly" and "perfectly done." The stairs in these areas are installed in a way that aligns logically with home access.
- **Substandard Execution**: A subset of homes exhibits major flaws. Steps are built "one way," described as "lazy" and nonsensical, forcing awkward entry patterns due to door swings. The concrete and hardscaping in these specific instances are also of lower quality, reinforcing the two-crew hypothesis.

### Key Deficiencies and Required Rework
The operational failures are concentrated in two primary areas, requiring immediate attention.

1. **Stair and Door Configuration**:
   - **The Problem**: Stairs on several units are built in the wrong direction, creating a direct conflict with the door swing and forcing users to walk around the landing to enter.
   - **The Cause**: The crew either failed to account for the door's orientation or deviated from the plan. In some cases, the stairs were intentionally turned to align with a different access path, but this was not coordinated with the door installation, creating a new problem.
   - **The Fix**: Two options exist: either fix the door swing to match the stairs or re-skirt the stairs to conceal the exposed wood and improve aesthetics, which is a cosmetic patch, not a functional solution.

2. **General Site and Infrastructure Issues**:
   - A new drain field may require a fence to prevent vehicles from driving over it.
   - Tree roots were severely damaged ("ripped the fuck out") during electrical work.
   - Minor punch list items include painting the office front door and replacing a missing cap on a post.
