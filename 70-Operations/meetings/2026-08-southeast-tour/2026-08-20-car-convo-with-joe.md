## Summary
A rolling conversation during a rehab tour on the Space Coast with a visitor, Joe. The rehab story is good: at least twelve completed recently with several already sold, units recovered through evictions turned around, work moving well in Georgia and South Carolina, and quality that stands out given some of them were hoarder houses. Financing is mostly cash buyers, Triad has inspected three or four financed homes, and one mortgage was denied because the community was not registered with the lender. Sales enquiries route through a central number and the NAV system so prospects are tracked, which raised the open question of what to do about on-site prospects who call the 24-hour line instead of walking into the office. Next work: the rehab team finishes in Silver and on the west coast, then moves south along the coast to the Thomas properties, replacing skirting, removing an unstable ramp and installing branded street signs to match the front signs; a home a 25-year renter has just left for a nursing home is next and needs floors. Two corporate items ran alongside: reserving 20 million dollars of capacity for a Bay Area deal, and a proposal to cut the equity commitment on opportunistic deals from 5 percent to 3.5 percent and reallocate the difference. A rocket launch at 11:19 interrupted proceedings.

## Decisions
- Keep selling to cash buyers while Triad works the financed files, and treat lender registration per community as a prerequisite — one denial has already been traced to it.
- Route all sales enquiries through the central number and NAV so every prospect is tracked.
- Next rehab route: finish Silver and the west coast, then work south along the coast to the Thomas properties.
- Scope on the next properties: replace skirting, remove the unstable ramp, and install new branded street signs matching the front signs.
