## Summary
A morning at the Florida Whispering Pines, where the booking system is quietly costing long-term revenue. Short-term bookings on Camp Spot are tying up lots meant for long stays, small units are landing on the large 50-amp sites, and the system currently will not let a long-term resident be booked past 2026. The fix agreed was minimum stay requirements on the sites historically used for full-season rentals — starting at six months with room to drop to three if bookings are slow — while the smaller west side sites stay open for short-term traffic. Rehab work was walked: finish the Daisy Lane kitchen, clean the carpets and repair the vanity; electrical problems on the Perry Winkle home after an eviction; new vinyl and appliances at 366 Lily Mora. New street signs are in progress and graveling is being checked. The heavy item is electrical: the utility reportedly requires a full community upgrade to move all services underground to current code before new homes go in, which hits roughly 64 of the 100 sites and costs materially more than pedestal upgrades, and the group questioned whether the utility should carry some of the cost of its own code change. The day ended in planning a tour for a visitor, Joe.

## Decisions
- Set minimum stays on the sites historically used for full-season rentals — start at six months, with flexibility down to three if bookings are slow — and keep the smaller west side sites open for short-term stays.
- Block the season sites in Camp Spot so small units stop occupying the large 50-amp lots.
- Rehab scope confirmed: finish the Daisy Lane kitchen, clean the carpets and repair the vanity; new vinyl flooring and appliances at 366 Lily Mora; electrical work on the Perry Winkle home post-eviction.
- Stub electrical toward likely future needs, for example near the dock, while the work is open.
