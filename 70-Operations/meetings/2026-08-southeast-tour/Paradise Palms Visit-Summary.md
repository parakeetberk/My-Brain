# Paradise Palms Visit

[image]

## Core Synopsis
Site observations reveal a basic execution failure in a manufactured-home park: stairs, door swings, concrete pads, and skirting are inconsistently installed—almost certainly by mixed crews—creating access conflicts, aesthetic degradation, and potential safety risks (e.g., exposed wood, mismatched step orientation, missing caps). The team notes correct concrete and stair execution across most units, with a minority showing reversed stair orientation aligned to door swings, implying an uncoordinated design-change midstream without downstream alignment of doors, skirting, and hardscape. The stakes are operational (user access flow is broken), reputational (visible quality lapses in a 55+ community), and liability (unprotected drain field and unskirted stairs), and the fix is straightforward but requires ownership: standardize the stair-door-skirting spec, enforce it at install, and remediate exceptions.

## Field Issues and Causal Map: Install Standards vs. Mixed Crews
- The Install Pattern Mismatch
  - Observed: “Night and day” differences in how stairs are set; some homes have stairs oriented to the back aligning with door swing; others do not.
  - Implication: Two crews or a mid-project spec change caused inconsistent application across identical home models.
  - Concrete: Most pads and steps in the park are “perfectly done”; outliers show execution drift (missing caps, exposed wood, misaligned steps).
- Door Swing and Access Flow
  - Mechanism: Door swing dictates functional stair orientation; when misaligned, users must “walk around,” introducing friction and accessibility issues.
  - Fix Logic: Either change door swing to match existing stair orientation or reorient stairs to match doors; in both cases, skirt stairs to hide wood.
- Aesthetics and Compliance (Hardscape/Skirting)
  - Observed: Exposed wood on stairs where skirting was not applied; inconsistent hardscape transitions near steps.
  - Standard: If stairs are the intended approach, skirting must be applied consistently to meet visual and likely community standards.
- Site Safety and Protection
  - Drain Field: Question raised about fencing the new drain field to prevent vehicles from driving onto it; current openness implies risk.
  - Electrical: Roots “ripped” for electrical—flagging rough utility trenching practices that may affect landscaping and tree health.
- Access Control and Operations
  - Code Issues: Entry codes not working for houses/clubhouse; team “snuck in,” indicating broken access control and process discipline.
  - Missing Components: “Missing a cap” callout suggests inventory or punch-list gaps not tracked to closure.
- Community Context
  - Demographic: 55+ community; quality, safety, and aesthetics are more sensitive, amplifying reputational impact of visible defects.
  - Sentiment: Team likes the park layout; quality outliers undermine the otherwise “cute” park feel.
