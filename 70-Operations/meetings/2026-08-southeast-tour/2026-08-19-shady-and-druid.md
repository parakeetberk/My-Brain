## Summary
A long combined session across Shady Acres and Druid Oaks. The operating picture: vacant former rentals concentrated on Front Street and State Street in poor condition, a renovated three bed three bath at 21 rented for 1,850 dollars, and unpermitted structures and additions being brought back into line with dollar violations, which is reportedly working. Hiring for maintenance is on its fifth attempt because previous hires did not want to work outdoors, so equipping the role properly with a Gator was floated as a retention move. The group agreed a sequencing rule that matters more than any single item: lot prep, tree work and clearing first, road work after, because the heavy equipment coming in for new homes would destroy new roads. Trees are the biggest physical job — pines dropping needles with shallow roots that fall in storms, several specifically lifting homes — alongside a pond needing overgrowth cut back and duckweed treated, with two alligators in it and an active EPD violation on the broken wall. Infrastructure decisions covered white three-rail PVC fence at every turn, a vinyl privacy fence at the lift station, standardized white PVC mailboxes with reflective numbering pending post office sign-off, and a solar motion camera at Druid where the new mailboxes have been broken into repeatedly. On the corporate side: a holdback from the GP portion against legal costs on the Habitat subpoena, and Rent Viewer being integrated with MAV to feed a central data warehouse, with MAV MCP expected in September.

## Decisions
- Send a resident memo by email and in print covering the engineering and septic work status, so the repeat questions stop.
- Sequence the work: lot prep, tree work and clearing first, road work last, because equipment for the new homes would tear up new roads.
- Install a second new sign at the back of the Druid property to match the new one at the front.
- Standardize mailboxes across the community on uniform single white PVC units with reflective numbering, pending consultation with the post office.
- Put a motion-activated solar camera with a light on the Druid mailboxes after repeated break-ins.
- Install white three-rail PVC fence reinforced with steel beams at every turn to keep vehicles off the grass, and a vinyl privacy fence around the lift station.
- Keep using dollar violations on unpermitted structures and additions — it is having an effect.
- Remove the pine trees whose roots are lifting homes.
- Hold back funds from the GP portion (initially "two and change") to cover potential legal costs on the Habitat subpoena before the distribution goes out.
- Integrate Rent Viewer with MAV systems into a central data warehouse for custom dashboard reporting, and protect the proprietary dashboard design in any call with the external party.
