## Summary
A handover conversation with the outgoing maintenance team, who speak both as departing staff and as residents. Their central point is that the current way of working is inefficient: a zero-turn mower would let one person get through the whole park, weekly weed spraying would get ahead of the persistent areas (especially the rubber mulch beds), and a small excavator would turn ditch and overgrowth clearing into five-second jobs instead of rental fees and outside contractors. The county holds right-of-way for ditch access, which complicates clearing, and residents have planted bushes and other items on land that is not theirs and resist having them removed. Equipment is the other theme: the Nissan truck cannot handle hauling drywall or front-end loader work, and the outgoing maintenance man argues that housing the maintenance staff on site produces better-kept grounds because the employee lives with the result. On enforcement, they describe having to get strict about road access after the park was fined when an ambulance could not get through, no standard day for resident lawn care, and sideways double-wides on two blocks that need proper tie-down. A storage shed still has no power because the county would not permit it.

- None recorded. The conversation was a handover briefing — the equipment purchases and the maintenance-housing argument were put forward as recommendations, and no commitment was made on the recording.
