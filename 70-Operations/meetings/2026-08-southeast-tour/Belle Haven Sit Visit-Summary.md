# Belle Haven Sit Visit

[image]

A group of stakeholders conducts a walkthrough of a mobile home park to assess its current state, including unit conditions, occupancy challenges, and resident issues. The discussion covers strategic initiatives for improvement, such as filling vacant lots with RVs, upgrading amenities like a pickleball court, and addressing maintenance issues with homes, windows, and infrastructure before a final meeting with staff to align on future plans.
---
## Current Property Conditions and Unit-Specific Issues
This section details the on-site assessment of the property's physical state, focusing on individual homes and infrastructure. The group observed that some units were "extra messy" during turnover, and certain doors lacked the structural provisions for top deadbolts, posing a security and repair challenge. A significant issue is the custom windows, which cost approximately $13,000 per house for a replacement and have a six-week lead time. While a "workable" fix has been found for some broken windows using standard replacements, it is not an ideal solution. Roof repairs were also a point of concern, with current patching efforts by contractors proving insufficient. The team agreed to move forward with full roof replacements, estimated at $5,000 to $7,000 per roof, rather than continue with temporary fixes. Infrastructure challenges include an entire back section of homes being connected to a single master meter and streetlights that, although functional, provide inadequate lighting.
---
## Occupancy Challenges and Resident Dynamics
This section addresses the challenges related to filling the park and managing resident relations. A primary hurdle is the affordability gap; while there is significant interest from potential residents, many are from a lower-income bracket and cannot afford the homes, sometimes due to being students. The group was surprised by their inability to secure eight long-term RV tenants from the large pool of local contractors. Historically, the park had a rate of around fifty sales per month when first purchased. The discussion also highlighted an interaction with a resident who detailed personal hardships, including caring for her terminally ill brother and experiencing a persistent water leak inside her trailer. She argued that since the park operates on a single master meter, the property owner is responsible for the leak, a point disputed by management who noted the home was purchased "as is." In contrast, the Riverwalk property was mentioned as a financially better-performing asset with high occupancy, though it has its own challenges with accounts receivable and evictions.
---
## Strategic Initiatives for Park Improvement and Marketing
This section outlines the strategic plans discussed to enhance the park's value and appeal. A key initiative is to improve community amenities by adding a pickleball court on the existing basketball court and a new dog park in the field. The group also decided to create a covered grill area to serve as a communal space for residents. To boost occupancy and revenue while awaiting new home deliveries, the team will target long-term RV tenants for vacant lots by launching a "block and blast" Facebook marketing campaign. This strategy is also seen as a way to upsell tenants to park models in the future. To test the sales market, a plan was proposed to purchase, skirt, and list a single RV for sale. Furthermore, the team will enhance the park's visibility and curb appeal by installing new fencing, signage, and "flyer flags" similar to those used successfully at the Fox Creek property.
---
## Operational Logistics and Staff Coordination
This section focuses on the operational and logistical aspects of managing the park, as well as coordination with on-site staff. The team commended the on-site manager, Julie, for her resourcefulness. She had proactively sourced an "abundance of windows" to circumvent the costly and time-consuming custom order process, and also found all the necessary mini blinds for the units by searching through the property's sheds. Additionally, she purchased shower curtains to improve unit presentation. The group met with Julie and Natalie in the office, noting how clean and improved the space was. The office area currently lacks designated parking, so staff parks near the office van. The clubhouse grill area, used for a recent cookout, is also temporarily being used for parking. The conversation reaffirmed the need to complete rehabs and address maintenance, with the understanding that major projects like road repaving would follow the finalization of city plans and new home installations.
