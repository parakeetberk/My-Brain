## Summary
The longest recording of the tour, and the one where the construction critical path is clearest. The model walk covered park models, single-wides and double-wides, including a custom 1437 that is effectively a one-bedroom with two lofts, and a single-wide praised for its pantry and two-bedroom layout. The clubhouse plan is a covered concrete pad for barbecue and picnic tables at one end and a closed room with a sink at the other; a real stove or oven is out because the space is classified commercial and would trigger hood and fire suppression requirements, gas is not allowed, and a 220 volt upgrade is not feasible. Permitting is the drag: Polk County fence permits vary by zoning clerk, Okeechobee runs a paper process requiring two copies of the install manual per packet with permits paid and physically collected within 72 hours or cancelled, and processing is three weeks behind. One inspector approved a pre-pour with a twelve-inch pipe matching the street standard and a second required fifteen inches and a specific slope, forcing demolition, re-pour and sod — six weeks lost. Better news: the flood zone question at the back resolved without elevation (20 foot flood against roughly 20.8 feet of grade plus set height), and the paving, curb and driveway scope was rebid from 1.8 million dollars to 683,000 dollars. Electrical is now the gate to occupancy — the inspector will not budge on matching a 200-amp home to a 200-amp pedestal, generators are not a workable substitute this phase, and Paradise Palms is close to energization. Sales planning ran alongside: stage one home per street, sell from outside the set units, fifty for-sale signs and twenty flags, and back-office capacity for twenty new home sales a month.

## Decisions
- Collect Okeechobee permits in person immediately on issuance, within the 72-hour window, so they are not cancelled.
- No elevation required at the back of the site — flood elevation is 20 feet against ground grade of 16 to 17 feet plus a standard set height of 3.8 feet, about 20.8 feet.
- Take the rebid paving, curb and driveway scope at 683,000 dollars, down from the 1.8 million dollar quote, and review the written estimate on receipt.
- Match pedestals to home ratings — a 200-amp home gets a 200-amp pedestal. The Southgate workaround of putting the AC disconnect outside the garage is not transferable to other jurisdictions.
- No generator solution this phase: a 3-phase generator plus connection points at roughly 12,000 dollars would change the wiring diagram and need permits that will not be granted. Small portable generators for a couple of fans during showings only.
- Set two NCS models and two park models as soon as permits land, even if the electrical hookups are still pending, using the staged viewing protocol.
- Stage one home on every street so buyers can see the range of floorplans, and sell from outside the units already set — four plans available.
- Sidewalks are three feet wide and grassed, running from the parking pad to the base of the home steps, installed after the CO is physically in hand.
- Stock each staged home with touch-up paint, dryer vent parts and appliance warranty booklets consolidated in a kitchen drawer.
- Tell buyers to live in the home ninety days before closing out the homeowner warranty punch list, so everything surfaces through daily use.
- Deploy fifty for-sale signs and twenty flags on site, and put flyer flags on end lots, negotiating with residents where the lot is not company-owned using 100 dollar Amazon gift cards.
- Run a bulk-purchase siding package program with credits on home purchases so owners can upgrade older units.
- Keep the relationship-driven approach with building officials — weekly calls — since expediters have declined mobile home permitting work as too small and too complex.
