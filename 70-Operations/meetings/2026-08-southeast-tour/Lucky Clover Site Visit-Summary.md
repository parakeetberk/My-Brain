# Lucky Clover Site Visit

[image]

This document outlines the key discussions from a site visit to a residential community, covering property improvements, operational challenges, and strategic planning. The conversation also branches into broader topics, including market trends, the impact of technology like AI on business and society, and long-term company strategy.

---
## Community Operations and Property Upgrades

During a tour of the "Lucky" community and others, the team assessed recent physical upgrades and planned future projects. The new roads and painted speed bumps were noted as significant improvements, with the fresh paving creating a visual illusion of wider roads, even though the dimensions remain unchanged. A key observation was that disruptive or "bad" residents had moved out during the improvement phase, leading to a more peaceful community environment. The conversation also covered strategies for managing resident issues, drawing on past experiences where direct, authoritative confrontation proved effective in resolving conflicts over rent with threatening tenants.

Future improvements were discussed in detail, focusing on the underutilized shuffleboard court area. Plans include either resurfacing the court or converting it to pickleball. The current sun shades over this area were identified as problematic; they sag, collect water during rain, and degrade quickly in the Florida climate, making a permanent metal roof a more durable alternative. To enhance security and usability, the team plans to install new lighting in the common area, powered by an external line connected to the dumpster area's photocell for automatic operation. Other planned upgrades include replacing a rotting perimeter fence and upgrading the community flagpole with a larger American flag and a secondary parakeet flag. To improve communication, new glass-enclosed bulletin boards will be installed behind every mailbox cluster, ensuring all residents see important notices in a secure, weatherproof display.

## Project Management and Development Strategy

The discussion centered on the strategic execution of large-scale community development projects, emphasizing the conversion of older properties to drive value. The strategy involves incentivizing residents to trade in older RVs or mobile homes for new, modern manufactured homes, which accelerates sales velocity and transforms the community's profile. A primary development goal is to transition parks from 50% RV occupancy toward 100% home occupancy, as referenced on the Newmark list.

The logistical workflow for home installation was detailed, involving coordination between Champion for setting the home and Cavco's team for the final trim-out and sealing. While the target timeline for setup after a home is delivered is 45 to 60 days, current data shows the team is averaging an efficient 37 days. Installation standards vary by jurisdiction; "soft set" procedures, which can be completed in about 15 days, are permissible for park models in some areas like River Oaks, while other locations demand full, lengthy permitting for all home types. Ongoing infrastructure work, such as abandoning old septic systems, continues in parallel. The high profitability of modular housing was highlighted with an example from Nantucket, where homes built for around $100,000 are selling for $5 million to $8 million. On the technology front, a key decision was made to centralize business process automation by creating dedicated server accounts for Power Automate, thereby restricting direct drive access and enhancing security.

## Personnel and Team Management

The conversation covered team dynamics, highlighting the intense challenges faced by on-site managers, including significant safety risks such as residents pointing guns at them. It was noted that a strong work ethic and internal drive are more critical for effective management than intelligence, though these qualities are perceived to be "dwindling dramatically" in the general workforce. The hiring process was touched upon with the example of Angel, who proactively applied for a manager position through the company website after visiting the communities.

The team's resilience and camaraderie were evident as they discussed managing volatile situations. A viewpoint was shared that difficult residents who resist community upgrades often leave on their own over time. In cases of direct threats, such as those over rent collection, experience shows that bold, decisive confrontation is more effective than passive approaches. The discussion also acknowledged that staffing is often impacted by personal events and bureaucratic hurdles, such as immigration paperwork issues. Finally, the group reflected on the current recruitment landscape for young professionals, describing it as a "miserable" and intensely competitive process starting as early as freshman year of college.

## Macro-Economic and Technological Trends

The speakers engaged in a broad discussion on external trends shaping business and society. Geopolitically, ongoing tensions in the Strait of Hormuz were noted. In the US housing market, a new law allowing for mortgage portability—where homeowners can transfer a low-interest rate (e.g., 3%) to a new property—was discussed as a significant potential stimulus. The high profitability of modular homes was highlighted again through the Nantucket example, where units costing $100,000 are selling for $5 million to $8 million. The conversation also touched on the perception that the US remains the primary destination for global investment despite economic uncertainties.

Technology, particularly AI, was a major focus. The speakers believe AI will significantly disrupt the job market, replacing roles in call centers and transportation (via self-driving vehicles). However, they warned against "AI slop," where reliance on AI without foundational knowledge degrades work quality. Conversely, AI can be a powerful productivity tool, especially for those with ADHD, by enabling non-linear thinking. The educational landscape is also adapting; the University of Michigan now requires college applicants to analyze their use of AI, teaching them to harness it responsibly. A decline in cursive handwriting instruction was lamented as potentially detrimental to cognitive development, as cursive promotes word-level brain processing. This led to a critique of the hyper-competitive academic environment, where children are prepped for interviews "like dogs," and the "brutal" recruitment process for elite finance jobs.

## Business Strategy and Industry Analysis

The discussion addressed high-level business strategy, focusing on optimizing financial performance and analyzing different industry models. It was noted that operating margins can be improved by passing through variable costs like city water to residents, directly increasing Net Operating Income (NOI). The outdoor hospitality sector was identified as a lower capital expenditure (capex) business model, with some operators generating 40-50% of their revenue from high-margin amenities like golf cart rentals and food, which benefit from a "captive audience." The strategy of owning a portfolio debt-free, exemplified by a company generating $12 million in NOI, was recognized as a powerful position.

To improve internal efficiency, the team plans to use AI for information extraction and administrative automation, such as generating leases. A key strategy is to build an internal AI agent that allows field managers to prompt a database from their phones to quickly retrieve information, like lot details, instead of navigating complex systems. This AI deployment will be integrated with the existing organizational chart, ensuring the automated tools have the same permission levels as the employees they assist. To maintain security, the company will use dedicated server accounts for Power Automate workflows, centralizing control and restricting broad data access.
