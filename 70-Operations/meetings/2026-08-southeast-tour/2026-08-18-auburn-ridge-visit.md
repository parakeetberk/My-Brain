## Summary
An afternoon walk of Auburn Ridge in Auburn, Georgia, mixing site items with water economics, a charity tenant, hiring and trip logistics. Roads are in good shape with about three speed bumps still needing paint, and the group talked through repurposing the old office structure as either a picnic area or a community library without landing on either. Water is the money story: a large share of spend goes on buying water from the city of Auburn, restoring the property well was judged too burdensome once treatment, testing and licensing are counted, and of the 128 lots only 1-58 are sub-metered with Metron meters while city meters run about 5,000 dollars each. With pipes buried five to six feet deep, leaks are nearly invisible, so installing a Metron meter downstream of the main city meter was proposed to catch supply-line losses. The team visited the Path Kids Club, a nonprofit renting a home on the property, left a 500 dollar gift card toward flooring, and heard from police that crime has dropped materially since the change of ownership. The rest of the visit covered a maintenance candidate interview, a golf cart for the distance, and a review of the make ready board and infill data that turned up a 70-day gap to get site surveys on some homes.

## Decisions
- Do not restore the property well — treatment, testing and licensing make it more burden than benefit; keep buying city water.
- Support the Path Kids Club with a donation toward new flooring; a 500 dollar gift card was handed over on site, and the organization keeps paying rent for its space. Amount and mechanism for the rest are still open.
- Buy a golf cart for the property, a utility model with a folding back seat, because the property is too large to cover on foot.
- Change the dashboard alert threshold for notices from 45 days to 60 days.
- Use the make ready board and infill data to set a performance baseline and improvement targets, starting with the time from permitting to home order.
