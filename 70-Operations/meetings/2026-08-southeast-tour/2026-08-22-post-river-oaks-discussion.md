## Summary
The debrief in the car after River Oaks, and it splits three ways. First, a grassroots marketing plan for River Oaks: send Vicky to communities whose homes sell well over 100,000 dollars and offer a referral fee for buyers looking for something cheaper, work the surrounding RV parks to learn where the snowbird traffic goes and drop River Oaks flyers, target retired veterans at the Elks Lodge and American Legion, and price the blank billboard on Bayshore Road at Route 41 to fix the visibility problem of a park that is tucked away. Second, a regional performance read across three regions: the South Carolina and Georgia region has improved the most, with teams that have turned a corner on knowledge; another region is stable but least improved, and the cleanliness found there — a dirty clubhouse and a dead cockroach — was called unacceptable; the newest regional impact since joining is positive against the prior management of that region. The core employee group was described as strong and loyal. Third, internal structure: three uninterrupted hours are needed to rewrite the SOPs, the accounting function ownership of utilities and operating permits was described as a black box with poor communication and should move to capable people outside accounting, and a long-tenured data and systems employee who wants into finance, asset management or acquisitions should stop being used to clean up other departments problems.

- Run the grassroots marketing play for River Oaks: referral fees from higher-priced communities, canvass the RV parks, distribute flyers, target the Elks Lodge and American Legion, and price the Bayshore Road and Route 41 billboard.
