## Summary
A walkthrough of Belle Haven in Clearwater, assessing unit condition, occupancy and resident issues before a closing meeting with staff. The physical problems are expensive: the custom windows run about 13,000 dollars a house with a six-week lead time, and roof patching by contractors is not holding, so the group agreed to move to full replacements at 5,000 to 7,000 dollars a roof. Some doors have no structural provision for a top deadbolt, an entire back section runs off one master meter, and the streetlights work but do not light much. Occupancy is the harder problem: interest is real but the affordability gap is real too, with many prospects in a lower income bracket and some still in school, and the team was surprised they could not land eight long-term RV tenants out of the local contractor pool. The plan to close the gap is to fill vacant lots with long-term RV tenants through a block and blast Facebook campaign while waiting on new home deliveries, then upsell to park models later, plus buying, skirting and listing a single RV to test the sales market. Amenity and curb appeal work: pickleball on the existing basketball court, a dog park in the field, a covered grill area, new fencing, signage and flyer flags on the Fox Creek model. On-site credit went to Julie, who sourced windows and mini blinds out of the property sheds rather than paying for custom orders.

## Decisions
- Move to full roof replacements at 5,000 to 7,000 dollars per roof instead of continuing with contractor patching.
- Target long-term RV tenants for the vacant lots while new home deliveries are pending, using a block and blast Facebook campaign, with an eye to upselling them to park models later.
- Buy one RV, skirt it and list it for sale to test the sales market.
- Add a pickleball court on the existing basketball court, a dog park in the field, and a covered grill area as a communal space.
- Install new fencing, signage and flyer flags, following what worked at Fox Creek.
- Hold road repaving until the city plans are final and the new homes are installed.
- Management position on the master-metered leak: the home was purchased as is, so the leak inside the trailer is not the owner responsibility.
