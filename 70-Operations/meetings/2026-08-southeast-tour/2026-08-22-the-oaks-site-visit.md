## Summary
A walk of The Oaks in Thonotosassa that produced more concrete decisions than most stops. The wastewater treatment plant is getting a 12-foot PVC fence in place of the chain link, installed before the new homes are listed so buyers do not see it, and partly because teenagers have been getting into the area. Trash is the recurring cost: staff spend four days a week picking up after overflow at Terrace Crossings, the pull-the-trash figure quoted was 15,000 dollars, and it is charged in rent tied to the upcoming increases — October here, July at Terrace Crossings. The preference is individual cans over a dumpster, at Terrace Crossings and Riverwalk both, because cans are easier to regulate and cut the cleanup. Maintenance capacity came next: a larger tough shed on a slab for tools and spare skirting, siding, PVC and fittings, a basic tool package to replace what left with the previous tool owner, and a gator for mobility. On amenities the group favoured expanding basketball with double hoops over adding pickleball, and a half-screened, half-open pavilion with electrical service modeled on Paradise Lakes. Resident engagement is strong — bingo, cookouts, pool movies, a committee of moms running events and one resident moderating the community Facebook page — and the group wanted both recognized. On tenure: three homes need rehab, no new incoming renters but renewals for existing ones, conversion of renters to owners, and buyout options on the legacy LOPs, about six of which are paid off.

## Decisions
- Replace the chain link around the wastewater treatment plant with a 12-foot PVC fence, installed before the new homes are listed.
- Move to individual trash cans rather than a dumpster, at Terrace Crossings and Riverwalk as well, because cans are easier to regulate and cut the four-days-a-week cleanup.
- Put a larger tough shed on a concrete slab to store tools plus spare skirting, siding, PVC and fittings, and buy a basic tool package.
- Expand basketball — re-stripe and add double hoops — rather than add pickleball, where interest looks low here.
- Build a half-screened, half-open pavilion with electrical service, modeled on Paradise Lakes, for events and mosquito relief.
- No new incoming renters; renew existing renters and keep converting renters to owners, with buyout options considered to accelerate LOP conversions.
- Keep shared pool and occasional clubhouse access for Terrace Crossings residents with the 100 dollar deposit applied to rent, and keep the access system and authorization controls secure.
- Connect the on-site team with the in-house financing specialist so prospects get walked through applications and forms, without signing for them.
- Provide lunch and tea for the resident committee meetings, and consider a small rent credit for the resident who moderates the community Facebook page.
- Proceed with next year budget preparation now — the timing is right.
- Lean toward buying a gator for on-site mobility; no final decision recorded.
