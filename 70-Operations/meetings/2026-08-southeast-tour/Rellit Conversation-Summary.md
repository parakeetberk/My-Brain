## Pain Points
The company's accounting department, led by a controller, is unable to provide timely financial data, resulting in books that are 30 to 45 days in arrears. This significant delay creates a "blind" spot in their financial operations. The impact is severe, as management cannot make quick, informed decisions to react to business challenges or opportunities. The current situation involves waiting over a month for financial reports, which is untenable for agile business management. For example, transactions made with Ramp credit cards are only booked once a month, whereas previously, Austin would review spend on a weekly basis. This lack of real-time expense tracking is particularly concerning given that many employees have company credit cards. The stakeholders most affected are the company leaders (Austin and John) who rely on this data for strategic decisions.
The existing property management software, Rent Manager, which handles their general ledger, does not offer the "live close" functionality they desire. While it is indispensable for managing resident ledgers and revenue—which is largely automated and live—it falls short on the expense and overall accounting side. This limitation is a core reason they are exploring new solutions. They recognize that an API could pull data from Rent Manager, but this introduces complexity and potential system overhaul. The core problem is the inability of their current system and processes to provide the real-time financial visibility needed to manage the business effectively.
## Expectations
The customer's primary expectation is to achieve a "live close" or real-time accounting environment. Their specific goal is to have financial data, particularly expenses, booked immediately as transactions occur, rather than at the end of the month. This would enable them to view the company's financial position live, "asap," allowing them to react, pivot, and solve problems more effectively. Success for this initiative would be measured by the elimination of the 30 to 45-day reporting lag and the ability to see accurate, up-to-date financial statements at any time. The stakeholders for this are the company leadership (Austin and John), who are the primary consumers of this financial data for business management. They are open to significant process changes and technology adoption to meet this goal.
The customer expects that a new solution will seamlessly integrate with their existing critical tools. They specifically mentioned needing an integration with Rent Manager for pulling ledger data and a native integration with Ramp for expense management. They are willing to facilitate an introduction to the Rent Manager team to help build an API connector, as Rent Manager has "tons of APIs" and partners with other integrators. The new system is expected to act as the "nucleus of the finance stack," with other tools feeding into it.
