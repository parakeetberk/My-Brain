## Summary
An evening walk of Gulf Breeze in Hudson, dominated by a problem that is entirely outside the company control: residents who bought homes cannot get water service turned on. One buyer who purchased four homes is affected, the water company explanations are inconsistent, and the resident has followed their instructions repeatedly with no result, while long-term residents appear to have service. The rest of the visit is inventory triage. There are multiple vacant sites and homes, including two FEMA homes, and before anything is scrapped the team wants to know whether the set homes are permitted and eligible for sale — options range from selling as-is after a new window, to marketing at a low price, to giving them away. Three newer park models (39, 40, 41) are set but water damaged, so a quick remodel or an as-is offering is preferred to get them occupied. The home on lot two behind the office is park-owned after an eviction, needs work, and already has an applicant. On vendors, the primary landscaper is underperforming badly enough that the on-site maintenance person is redoing the work, so buying a zero-turn and a small trailer to bring it in-house was proposed, along with including lawn care for all residents to get uniform maintenance.

## Decisions
- Every applicant, investors included, pays the 50 dollar application fee and a deposit; the fee is refunded if they pass the background check and complete the purchase.
- Salvage the good appliances out of any home that gets scrapped, for use in other rehabs.
- Verify permitting and sale eligibility on the set FEMA homes before deciding whether to scrap, sell as-is, price low or give away.
- Quick remodel or an as-is offering on park models 39, 40 and 41 despite the water damage, to get them occupied.
- Keep background checks as the gate for anyone entering the community, investors included.
