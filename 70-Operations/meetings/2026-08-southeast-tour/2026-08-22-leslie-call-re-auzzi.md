## Summary
A call about the person who owns the sales system and his expectation of a flat commission on every deal on the grounds that he architected it. The others disagree: commission should follow direct involvement, such as leads generated through the system he runs, and his original structure dates from when he was actually making calls and working prospects, which is no longer the job. A review of the system found real hygiene problems — about 700 aged-out prospects and 84 tours in the wrong category — which sits badly against the claim that the system is working. The group also separated the words from the thing: because payouts get held back when process is not followed, what is being paid is a discretionary bonus, not a commission, and it should be formalized as a written performance-based bonus policy tied to metrics like system effectiveness. That would also settle the pushback from another employee over withheld commissions. Whether he fits the role at all is unresolved — the role needs a customer success mindset and an understanding that engagement takes many touches, and his approach was described as set it and forget it — so the plan is two weeks of direct observation in the Miami office before any decision, with the fallback that he returns to a broader jack-of-all-trades role rather than leaving.

## Decisions
- Commission follows direct involvement; there is no flat commission on every deal in the company.
- Convert the arrangement from commission to a written, performance-based bonus policy tied to metrics such as system effectiveness, and apply it consistently across everyone on a commission-style payout.
- Ask him to sign his official job description.
- If the role is not a fit, he moves back to a broader jack-of-all-trades role rather than being exited.
- Assess him in the Miami office over the next couple of weeks before deciding anything.
- Ownership of what happens inside the system is his, including collaborating with whoever else is needed to keep it clean.
