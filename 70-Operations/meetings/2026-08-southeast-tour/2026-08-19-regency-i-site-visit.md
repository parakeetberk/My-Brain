## Summary
First stop of 2026-08-19, in Savannah. The property is visibly cleaner and calmer since the change of ownership, and being able to enforce fines for violations has moved residents to clean up their lots; the inspector acknowledged the licensing process is under way so operations continue. The new entrance sign has been delivered and sits in the shop waiting on installation, with a white pole planned and decorative elements later — complicated by the city owning the land in front of the electrical pole under an easement. The bigger items are the mailboxes, which are falling apart and already approved for replacement pending a vendor, and the dumpster area, which costs 395 dollars a month and keeps collecting tires and bulk waste; the group wants the dumpster gone and the space turned into trailer storage. Landlords are a live theme: there are somewhere between 10 and 30 of them, the engaged ones like Mr. Butler run well-kept lots, and one named Larry holds close to 20 units at rents as high as 2,000 dollars. Remaining project list: tree trimming before hurricane season, an ADA-compliant bathroom, road work on Green Oak, a parking light project in progress, and 14 to 25 vacant lots of which many are too small to fill without moving existing homes.

## Decisions
- Remove the dumpster and its 395 dollar a month service, clear the container of tires, and convert the space to trailer storage.
- Proceed with the already-approved mailbox replacement: larger structures with better weather protection; the newer metal ones on Green Oak stay.
- Install the new entrance sign on a white pole with decorative elements to follow, sited to respect the city easement.
- Get the tree trimming done before hurricane season, for appearance and safety.
- Start the road work on Green Oak with patching and seal coating, and evaluate which sections need repaving.
- Make one bathroom ADA compliant.
