# Vantage Oaks Site Visit

[image]

The group conducted an in-depth property walk and operational review spanning model home inspections, clubhouse planning, permitting and inspection hurdles, electrical and utility readiness, and a coordinated sales/marketing ramp. ## Property Walkthrough and Home Inspection

The team toured multiple models (park models, single-wides, double-wides) and discussed layout suitability and amenity planning. A custom “1437” model was described as essentially a one-bedroom with two lofts—bunk beds under the taller loft and a small bedroom under the shorter loft—highlighted as flexible space for families with babies or grandkids. Several homes feature darker, rustic wood-look flooring distinct from standard packages. In the single-wide inspected, the kitchen drew praise for a large pantry and two-bedroom layout with dedicated bathrooms and closets.

The clubhouse plan is to create a community hub: one end will be a covered concrete pad for barbecue and picnic tables; the other end will be a closed room with a sink to function as a small kitchen. The clubhouse roof was recently replaced; however, installing traditional stove/oven equipment would trigger commercial hood and fire suppression requirements due to the space’s commercial classification. Gas is not allowed at the facility, and an upgrade to 220 volts for kitchen equipment is considered not feasible.

Sidewalks are planned as three feet wide, grassed, and routed from the parking pad to the base of the home steps. Each staged home is stocked with spare touch-up paint, dryer vent parts, and warranty booklets consolidated in a kitchen drawer for appliances (fridge, stove). The practice is to advise residents to live in the home for ninety days before completing the homeowner warranty punch list, ensuring that all issues surface through daily use. Minor warranty items were noted (e.g., a chipped drawer front) to be handled via replacement parts from the manufacturer.

## Permitting and Construction Challenges

Permitting complexities are prominent, with jurisdiction-specific rules and inconsistent inspector standards driving delays. In Polk County, fence replacements on commercial property can require permitting and reviews vary significantly by zoning clerk—some rigorously question submittals while others approve the same documentation. The Okeechobee Building Department maintains especially stringent, paper-based processes: every permit packet must include two copies of the home’s installation manual, permits must be paid for and physically picked up within seventy-two hours of issuance, and electronic systems were only recently adopted. To avoid cancellation, the team will personally collect permits immediately upon issuance.

Flood zone concerns at the back of the site were resolved: flood elevation is twenty feet, and the combination of ground grade (sixteen–seventeen feet) plus standard set height (3.8 feet) yields approximately 20.8 feet, eliminating the need for elevation. Paving, curbs, and driveways were rebid successfully—cost reduced to $683,000 from a $1,800,000 quote. However, inspections caused significant rework: one inspector approved a pre-pour with a twelve-inch pipe (matching the street’s standard), while a subsequent inspector required a fifteen-inch pipe and specific concrete slope, forcing demolition and re-pour and adding sod to slopes, resulting in a six-week delay. Permitting processing is reported as three weeks behind, but all necessary permits for current work are filed. Expediters declined mobile home permitting engagements, considering them too small and complex compared to large commercial projects—reinforcing a relationship-driven approach with building officials (weekly calls with contacts like Gage and Kevin) to navigate local nuances.

## Electrical and Utility Installation Status

Electrical readiness is the critical path to occupancy. Kevin, the inspector, insists that any house rated at 200 amps must have a matching 200-amp pedestal; he will not deviate from that point. At Southgate (with 100-amp pedestals), the team avoided upgrades by placing the AC disconnect outside the garage, but this workaround is not broadly acceptable in all jurisdictions.

Paradise Palms is nearing energization: all new pedestals are connected, Champion is scheduled to connect homes to pedestals the following week, and the city is slated to complete their portion then; energization is expected by the end of the week or by the following weekend at the latest. Electrical contractors have completed boring under the road and were restoring asphalt during the visit. The team flagged the risk of having homes set, surveyed, and trimmed without available electric meters, causing pauses in readiness.

Using large generators to power full 200-amp homes is not viable while the homes are commercially owned; adding generator connection points would change the wiring diagram and require permits that will not be granted. A full generator solution would run approximately $12,000 (3-phase generator plus required connection points) and remains prohibited for the current phase. For open houses, the plan is to use small portable generators to power a couple of fans and open windows—strictly for minimal comfort during showings—until permanent power is connected. Separately, written estimates for paving/curbs/driveways will be reviewed upon receipt, and kitchen/stove outlet issues will be coordinated with Chris for near-term resolution.

## Sales, Marketing, and Community Improvement Strategy

The sales strategy emphasizes staging and visibility. The company owns all homes on certain streets, enabling staged inventory without resident interference; the decision was to stage one on every street to showcase options across floorplans. The current plan also includes selling from outside units that are already set, offering four different home plans while internal access remains limited in some doubles.

Aggressive on-site marketing is planned using fifty “For Sale” signs and twenty flags, with additional flyer flags placed at end-lots (negotiated with residents via $100 Amazon gift card offers where lots are not company-owned). Direct resident engagement and street-end visibility proved effective in prior communities. A siding package program is proposed—bulk purchasing and credits on home purchases—to help owners upgrade older units and improve aesthetics. Removing dilapidated/vacant lots has improved community sentiment, and two “drug houses” were previously removed. The clubhouse roof was redone, and sidewalk installations will proceed post-CO issuance. Management is preparing for a throughput of twenty new home sales per month; the back-office must scale to handle closings, titles, and paperwork alongside an existing pipeline of approximately 500 used-stock sales to be reduced over time.
