## Summary
Two very different halves in one recording. The first is an on-site review at Fox Creek that reads as a turnaround: a new mowing crew cleared two months of overgrowth in two days, the new maintenance worker is described as night and day against the former staff, and tighter screening with credit and background checks has moved out the troublemakers. Sales are being pushed as-is rather than through expensive remodels to hit buyer price points, with new homeowners told plainly that the exterior — roof coating, pressure washing, skirting — is theirs to maintain. Physical plans discussed were three-rail fencing on the entrance corner, a covered gazebo-style bus stop with seating, replacement mailbox signage with a bulletin board, and new unit number markers. A phone leg covers a contract extension held up by two clauses — a release of responsibility on six homes and language questioning the validity of a debt — which the speaker wants struck, with a principals meeting to settle the business terms instead of more lawyer-to-lawyer traffic. The last half is a pitch of Corner IQ, a model-agnostic MCP memory layer, which the team judged interesting mainly for the multi-model gateway and the shared context layer, and agreed to test through a one-month trial with a small executive group before any money changes hands.

## Decisions
- Prioritize as-is home sales over expensive remodels so the price point matches what buyers can actually finance.
- Keep annual lot rent increases and be upfront with applicants that they are standard practice in the South Carolina market.
- Put three-rail fencing along the entrance corner, sited to avoid tree removal and to preserve county access to the water utilities.
- Build a covered, gazebo-style bus stop with integrated seating, which also stops residents sheltering under the mailbox overhang.
- Replace the peeling mailbox signage, add a bulletin board for community notices, and add professional unit number markers.
- Strike the two contested clauses from the contract extension and get the principals from both sides in a room to close the remaining business terms.
- Take the one-month Corner IQ trial with a small group of executive leaders as a discovery phase, before any financial commitment.
