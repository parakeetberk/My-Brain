# Money flow

**What this is:** how cash comes into Parakeet and how it goes out — the revenue lines, the fixed monthly collections-and-close calendar that is the real rhythm of the business, the payables and payroll rails, and the one structural problem the owner names as top-three.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

### Money in

- **Lot rent is the core line, billed monthly in Rent Manager.** Residents run auto-pay through **Tenant Web Access**; Rent Manager holds the recurring charges, billing schedules and the AR report the whole collections calendar keys off. (Source: [[10-Business/vendors/rent-manager]].)
- **Utility reimbursements are billed pro rata on a total lot basis, regardless of whether a lot is vacant or occupied**, so reimbursement income rises with occupancy rather than tracking consumption. (March 2026 board deck, JREFI 2026 budget commentary.)
- **Home sale proceeds arrive on three routed paths** — cash, financed through consumer lenders, or **Lease with Option to Purchase**, which pays in monthly instalments rather than a lump sum. The Closing Coordinator routes every deal down one of the three. (Source: [[50-Products-Sales/home-sales]].)
- **RV revenue comes in as transient and seasonal bookings taken in Campspot**, with Rent Manager still holding the asset and the ledger. The March 2026 deck treats Campspot as a revenue line, not just a booking tool: the 2026 RV goal is to in-fill RV sites with park models and "generate incremental revenues from Campspot."
- **Ancillary income:** boat slips at Triple S Marina, the **Geiger Key Fish Camp restaurant**, and community fees. Geiger Key restaurant income was reported separately in the JREFI 2025 statements ($3,592,791 of restaurant income against $20,160,650 of rental income, income-tax basis, year to 2025-12-31; March 2026 board deck appendix).

### The monthly calendar — collections and close

This is the operating rhythm of the month. Dates are as the SOPs state them; where two SOPs disagree, both are shown.

- **1st** — Community/District Manager pulls the AR report in Rent Manager, prioritises it, and starts the reminder sequence: reminder email on the 1st, follow-up on the 3rd, email **and** text in an urgent tone on the 5th. (SOP 100.)
- **6th** — state-specific demand notices are produced in Rent Manager, then posted and mailed the same day. **Proof of posting and certified-mail tracking are with the Regional Manager by end of day on the 6th**, and the RM's compiled proofs go to the Director of Operations and evictions@parakeetcommunities.com on the 7th. **Who produces the notices is recorded two ways** — the Operations Coordinator in SOP 100, the Evictions Team in SOP 107. (Sources: [[70-Operations/sops/100-ar-and-collections]], [[70-Operations/sops/107-eviction]].)
- **8th to the cure date** — daily AR review and resident calls. Intake packets are generated and sent to Regional Managers on the **19th–23rd** and returned completed **by the 25th**.
- **25th** — the Rent Roll Summary Report (PDF) is sent to all Community Managers, who mark needed updates directly on the PDF. (SOP 301.)
- **27th** — the Month-End JotForm is due at **12:00 PM**; if the due date falls on a weekend or holiday it moves to the preceding business day. Regional Managers save the PDF reports to the month-end closeout folders on the 27th–28th and record updates in the **Month End Tracker Report**. (SOP 110 checklist, SOP 301.)
- **29th** — the **Director of Operations reviews and approves the Month End Tracker**, which releases the month-end data for entry in Rent Manager.
- **Last week of the month** — completed intake packets are forwarded to Evictions and on to the attorney. An incomplete intake at month end is deferred to the following month's cycle.
- **The calendar contradicts itself on the month-end deadline.** SOP 110's body, FAQ and tracking table say **12:00 PM on the 25th**; its own header table and the checklist Community Managers actually sign say **the 27th with DOO sign-off on the 29th**. The 27th/29th version is the one that lines up with SOP 301; the 25th version asks a CM to close the month using a report that arrives the same day. Unresolved as of 2026-08-30.

### Who chases what

- **Community and District Managers execute collections** — the emails, texts, calls, notice posting and Rent Manager History & Notes documentation.
- **The Regional Manager is the sole approver of a payment stipulation.** A CM or DM may prepare and present one but must not communicate approval before RM sign-off.
- **The Evictions Team runs the notices and all attorney communication**, and only closes a workflow on written attorney confirmation.
- Attorney fees must be posted to the account before any payment is accepted to stop an eviction, and the resident pays by **cashier's check or money order only**, mailed to the home office.

### Money out

- **Every vendor invoice is NET 30, paid through AVID Exchange.** If a vendor is unpaid at NET 30, Accounting checks AVID. (Source: [[70-Operations/sops/300-avid-exchange-ap]].)
- **Invoice approval routing:** CapEx (construction, infrastructure, capital repairs) → **John Clark, at any amount**; park-owned-home spend **over $1,000** → **Leslie Croft**; everything else, including POH spend **at or under $1,000** → the **Standard Regional Workflow** to the Director of Operations. A $950 POH invoice stays standard.
- **The $1,000 POH threshold has no companion control** — nothing prevents splitting a job into sub-$1,000 invoices, and no SOP defines what counts as one expenditure.
- **Field spend runs on Ramp cards, and every charge must carry a GL code in the memo field**, entered immediately, in the format `GL [XXXX] — [vendor or description]`. Home Depot and Lowe's charges additionally carry the rehab Issue number and the lot. There is no petty cash and no purchase-order process documented anywhere. (Source: [[10-Business/vendors/ramp]].)
- **Payroll:** the workweek runs **Monday to Sunday**; team members are paid **biweekly, every other Friday, through Paychex**. Overtime is **1.5× over 40 hours in a workweek** and **must be pre-approved in writing** — unauthorised overtime is a disciplinary matter. Holidays, vacation and sick days are excluded from the 40-hour calculation.

### The live problem

- **Transactions are not booked until month-end, which leaves the company 30 to 45 days in arrears with no live spend visibility. The owner names this as one of his top three problems.** Ramp spend that used to be reviewed weekly is now reviewed once a month while cards are held widely across the company — "so it's kind of like blind." (2026-08-21 Rellit conversation.)
- **The same function is described as not communicating outward.** On 2026-08-22, on where utilities and operating permits actually sit: **"There's a black box. It doesn't communicate."** Stated at function level, not as a verdict on any person.
- The stated target is a live close — "when people are booking things or putting their memos and allocations in Ramp, it should be booked right away. And therefore we know where we are."

### What has improved, per the March 2026 board deck

- **Financial reporting turnaround shortened from two weeks to under 48 hours** through automation (Asset Management wins, six months to March 2026).
- **About $40k of annual like-for-like savings from renegotiated payment processor contracts** (Accounting & Finance wins).
- **WIP accounts and codes instituted to track CapEx by asset, property and project**; utility bill payment and processing outsourced.
- **Real-time cash flow reporting and future cash projections is a Q2 2026 rock held jointly by David Berman and Zach Schwartz.** The Rocks file places it in Q2; the March deck labels the same item Q3 and lists it as a department goal ("REAL-TIME CASH REPORTING — Monthly internal cash planning"). No evidence of delivery is in the vault. (Source: [[10-Business/eos/rocks-2026]].)
