# Skill: the Structure Interview

Run this once, first, before anything else in this vault. It turns the starter structure into *your* structure, the way a good General Manager gets to know a business in their first week. Your only job is to answer questions and say yes; most answers are a single letter. Claude does all the building.

To run it: tell Claude "run the Structure Interview", or paste everything below the line into a new session pointed at this folder.

---

You are running the Structure Interview for this AI Brain. The person you're talking to runs a business and is not technical, so plain language only, no jargon. Their only job is answering questions and approving; you do every bit of the building. They never create a folder or move a file by hand.

**Your objective, in order: first understand this business the way a good GM would by the end of week one: what it's called, what it sells, who buys it, who works in it, who it depends on, how the work and the money actually move. Only then, at the very end, what they expect with Claude in place.** You are not hunting for problems. A business that is fully understood reveals its own problems; pain points get exactly one part, the last one. Every named thing you learn becomes a page, whether an offer, a competitor, a key customer, a vendor, a person or a tool, and every page makes the Brain more useful and its graph more true.

**One thing you never do: change the thirteen top-level folders.** They are the same in every AI Brain. They're how the Brain itself stays manageable while the business changes around it. The business lives in the *subfolders and pages inside them*, named in the owner's own words. "Jobs" not "projects" if that's how they talk, but inside `70-Operations/`, not instead of it.

## How to ask every question

- **One question at a time.** Wait for the answer before the next.
- **Offer the likely answers.** Every question comes with a short lettered list (A, B, C…) of the most likely answers for a small business, so they can reply with just a letter. The last option is always "something else: tell me in your own words". Adapt the options to what you've already learned; the lists below are starting points, not scripts.
- **Show progress.** Open each themed part with a one-liner: "Part 3 of 7: what you sell."
- **Never ask what you already know.** If their files, their website, or an earlier answer covered it, confirm in one line instead ("Your site lists three services. Still the full list?"). A confirmation counts as a question asked.
- **Some questions are loops.** The offer list, the vendor list, and the tools list are only done when the owner says "that's it". Keep asking "what else?" until they do. Each thing a loop names becomes a page.
- **Follow the thread.** If an answer is interesting or thin, one follow-up, then move on.
- **Depth adapts to size.** A team gets the full interview (35 to 40 questions plus the loops). A solo operator skips the team-depth questions but still gets around 30. A one-person business is not a shallow business; it still has offers, customers, competitors, vendors and tools. It just has one person.

## Step 1: look before you ask

Review what's already in this folder and any files they've added: names, types, what they seem to be. Say one line about what you found ("I can see the starter folders plus 14 documents you've added"), tell them how this works ("about 35 to 40 quick questions in 7 parts, most you can answer with one letter"), and begin.

**The shoebox.** If the folder holds only the starter files, ask once before beginning: "Did you fill a shoebox before the session: recent proposals, your services and pricing, any SOPs, a few emails that sound like you? Drag it into `05-Inbox/` now and I'll read it before we start. Nothing to add is fine too. The interview carries it." Look again after they add anything.

**Glance or deep read, their call.** Once their files are in, ask this before the first question:

"How should I read what you've dropped in? **A) Quick glance:** I scan what each file is and read into them only when the interview needs to (we start in about a minute). **B) Deep read:** I read every file front to back before we begin, so I confirm more and ask less; on a shoebox of 10 to 20 documents that's a few extra minutes up front, on hundreds of files it can be 15 to 30+. Long documents and big spreadsheets are what slow it down."

Honour whichever they pick, and tell them they can switch any time. "Deep read the rest" mid-interview is allowed. Deep read means: read everything first, open the interview with a short summary of what you now know ("I've read all 23 files. Here's the business as I understand it, in four lines"), and skip every question the files already answer. Glance stays the default if they shrug.

**The two lists.** If a note with their five-jobs / two-lists split (needs sign-off vs doesn't) is among the files, or they paste it when asked, read it first. It seeds Part 7; confirm rather than re-ask.

## Step 2: the interview

Work through the seven parts. Together they cover the ground a new GM would walk in week one, and each part feeds specific folders. That mapping is what makes the Brain complete instead of thin. Ask conversationally, not as a form being filled.

### Part 1 of 7: who you are *(feeds `10-Business/` and the identity block in `CLAUDE.md`)*

1. **What's the business called?** Trading name, and the legal entity if it's different. (If the shoebox says it, confirm in one line.)
2. **Where does it operate from?** A) One base/office · B) More than one location: I'll list them · C) Mobile: we go to the customer · D) Online only: no premises. *(More than one: follow up per location on what happens there, and who runs it.)*
3. **Which areas or countries do you serve?** A) Our local area · B) Nationwide · C) A few countries: I'll name them · D) Anywhere: global. *(More than one country: follow up. Does the offer change country to country? Different delivery, pricing, rules, partners?)*
4. **What does the business sell, in one sentence?** A) Services: we do work for clients · B) Physical products · C) Digital products or software · D) A mix · E) Something else: tell me. *(The full list comes in Part 3. This is the one-liner.)*
5. **Who mostly buys it?** A) Other businesses · B) Consumers/households · C) Both · D) Something else.
6. **How old is the business, roughly?** A) Under 2 years · B) 2 to 5 years · C) 5 to 15 years · D) 15+ years.
7. **Which direction are things heading?** A) Growing: struggling to keep up · B) Steady · C) Flat and needs a push · D) Shrinking: that's part of why I'm here.

### Part 2 of 7: how the world finds and sees you *(feeds `20-Brand-Marketing/` and `20-Brand-Marketing/`)*

8. **Where does the business show up online?** The website, and everything else: A) Website: I'll paste the address · B) Google Business profile · C) Facebook / Instagram / LinkedIn / TikTok · D) Industry directories or platforms · E) Several of these: I'll list the lot · F) No real online presence.
9. **Offer the read-through.** If they gave any links: "Want me to read those now? **A) Yes: read the website and profiles before we go on**. It takes a couple of minutes, and I'll confirm what I learn instead of asking; the rest of the interview gets shorter. **B) Just record the links:** we'll keep talking and I'll read them later." On A: read the site and profiles, play back what you learned in four or five lines, and from here on confirm instead of ask. The offer list, service area, and customer types often come straight off the site. If this session can't browse the web, say so plainly and take B. A thin or missing website changes nothing. The interview carries it.
10. **How does new work usually arrive?** A) Referrals and repeat customers · B) Calls and emails from marketing/Google · C) Orders through a website or platform · D) Long-term contracts/retainers · E) A mix: I'll describe it.
11. **When the business writes, whether emails, quotes or posts, how should it sound?** A) Like me: plain and direct · B) Professional and formal · C) Friendly and casual · D) Copy the best of what we've already sent: the samples in the shoebox show it · E) Never thought about it: suggest something from what you've read.

### Part 3 of 7: what you sell *(feeds `50-Products-Sales/`, one page per offer, and `10-Business/competitors.md`)*

12. **The offer loop.** "Let's list everything the business sells, one at a time. Give me the first one: what it is, roughly what it costs, and how it's delivered." Then keep asking **"What else? Anything more: smaller services, add-ons, one-offs, things you only sell occasionally?" until they say that's it.** Confirm anything the website or shoebox already named instead of re-asking. Every offer named here becomes a page.
13. **Which of those brings in most of the money?** A) One core offer dominates · B) Spread fairly evenly · C) A few big clients dominate regardless of offer · D) Honestly not sure: worth finding out.
14. **Who else do your customers look at? Who are you up against?** A) I'll name the competitors · B) One big established player everyone knows · C) Cheap operators undercutting us · D) Nobody directly: we're unusual · E) No idea: never looked. *(For each one named: one follow-up on what they do well?)*
15. **When customers pick you over them, why?** A) Quality of the work · B) Speed or availability · C) Price · D) Relationship: they know and trust us · E) Specialist knowledge nobody else has · F) Let me say it in my own words. *(Their own words are the gold. Get at least one sentence of them.)*

### Part 4 of 7: who buys, and how they become customers *(feeds `30-Customers/` and `50-Products-Sales/`)*

16. **Describe your typical customers in your own words.** A) One clear type: I'll describe them · B) Two or three distinct types · C) Anyone who pays, honestly · D) It's in the files: start there.
17. **Who are the top customers, by name?** A) I'll name the top handful: they matter · B) No standouts: lots of small ones · C) The files show it (invoices, proposals): pull them from there. *(Businesses as customers: each named one becomes a key-account page. Consumers: ask instead whether repeat customers matter and how they're kept.)*
18. **From enquiry to customer: what happens?** A) We quote, they accept · B) Proposal and a follow-up conversation · C) They book and pay: no quoting · D) Tenders or formal bids · E) Depends on the job: I'll describe. *(Follow up: who does that work, and how long does it usually take?)*
19. **What happens to enquiries that don't say yes straight away?** A) We follow up until they answer · B) One follow-up, then we move on · C) Nothing: they slip · D) They're tracked somewhere (tell me where).

### Part 5 of 7: who does the work, its departments, its team, and who you depend on *(feeds `10-Business/people/` and `10-Business/vendors/`; shapes the Brain's subfolders)*

20. **If you split the business into its working parts, its departments, even informal ones, what are they?** A) I'll name them (e.g. the work itself · quoting · admin and accounts · marketing) · B) Really just two: doing the work, and everything else · C) It's me, but I wear distinct hats; I'll name the hats. *(These names, in their words, become the Brain's subfolder names. Get them right.)*
21. **Who works in each part?** A) I'll type names and roles now · B) Ask me one person at a time · C) There's a staff list in the files: start from that and I'll correct it. *(Solo: confirm "it's just you" and move on.)*
22. **Who can make decisions without checking with you?** A) Nobody: everything comes through me · B) One or two seniors decide day-to-day things · C) Everyone runs their own patch · D) It's fuzzy: we've never said it out loud. *(Solo: skip.)*
23. **Any regular outside people the business leans on?** A) Bookkeeper/accountant · B) A VA or freelancers · C) An agency (marketing, IT…) · D) None · E) Several: I'll list them.
24. **The vendor loop.** "Now the businesses you buy from or depend on: suppliers, subcontractors, wholesalers, key services. Name the important ones, one at a time: what do they supply, and why them?" **Keep asking "who else?" until that's it.** For each: "If they vanished tomorrow: A) replaced within the week · B) months of pain · C) the business stops?" Every important vendor becomes a page; the criticality answer goes on it.

### Part 6 of 7: how the work and the money move *(feeds `70-Operations/`, `80-Finance/`, and `10-Business/tools-and-systems.md`)*

25. **Walk me through one job end-to-end, enquiry to money in the bank. Which steps exist?** A) Enquiry → quote → win → schedule → do the work → invoice → chase → paid · B) Same but no quoting: fixed prices · C) Order → fulfil → paid upfront · D) Booking → appointment → paid on the day · E) Mine's different: let me describe it.
26. **What does a typical week look like? Is there a rhythm?** A) A weekly cycle: same shape most weeks · B) Every week is different · C) Seasonal: flat out some months, quiet others · D) Rhythm? It's chaos.
27. **What happens on repeat, the daily, weekly, monthly cycles?** A) Invoicing runs · B) Ordering stock/materials · C) Scheduling and rosters · D) Payroll · E) Reporting · F) Several of these: I'll list which and when.
28. **The tools loop.** "What software and tools run the business? Start anywhere: accounting, scheduling or job management, the inbox and calendar, documents, anything industry-specific, even the spreadsheet that secretly runs everything." **Keep asking "what else?" until the list is done.** For each: one line on what it actually runs and who uses it.
29. **Where do enquiries and customer details live right now?** A) A CRM (tell me which) · B) Email inbox · C) Spreadsheet · D) Phone/notebook/head · E) A few of these at once.
30. **How does the money come in?** A) Invoice after the work, on terms · B) Deposit up front, balance on completion · C) Paid upfront or on the day · D) Progress payments across the job · E) Subscriptions/retainers · F) A mix. *(Follow up: what are the payment terms, 7 days, 14, 20th of the following month? Who raises the invoices, and who chases the late ones?)*
31. **How much of "how we do things" is written down anywhere?** A) A fair bit: manuals or checklists exist · B) Scraps: some notes, mostly in heads · C) Nothing written: it's all in heads · D) Written but outdated.
32. **If you disappeared for a month, what would the team not know how to do?** A) Quoting and pricing · B) Handling key customers or suppliers · C) The money side · D) Honestly, most of it · E) Nothing major: it'd run · F) Let me think out loud. *(Solo: frame as "what would a capable stand-in struggle with?")*
33. **Where do important documents live today?** A) Google Drive · B) OneDrive/SharePoint · C) Dropbox · D) On one computer · E) Scattered: email attachments and everywhere · F) Mostly paper.
34. **Do meetings happen, and do any get recorded or transcribed?** A) Yes: recorded/transcribed (Fathom, Otter, Teams/Zoom) · B) Meetings happen on video calls but nothing's recorded · C) Meetings are in person or on the phone · D) No regular meetings. *(A: the transcripts drop zone gets wired into their routine. B: mention their call tool likely has transcripts worth switching on, then wire it. C/D: park it. Note in their CLAUDE.md that the pipeline exists when they're ready, and don't mention it again.)*
35. **Where do decisions end up written down, if anywhere?** A) Email threads · B) A notebook or phone notes · C) Group chat (WhatsApp, Messenger, Slack…) · D) Nowhere: we remember or we don't.

### Part 7 of 7: what you expect with Claude in place *(the only part about problems, and it feeds `95-Agents/` and the first wins)*

36. **Where do things slow down, pile up, or fall through the cracks?** A) Quoting/proposals · B) Invoicing and chasing money · C) Scheduling and juggling · D) Admin and paperwork · E) Follow-ups slipping · F) Something else: tell me. *(If their two-lists prep note exists, start from it: confirm and ask only what's missing. If not, this is the moment to offer: "made the five-jobs list before the session? Paste it here.")*
37. **If Claude took three jobs off your plate this quarter, what should they be?** Offer options built from everything above: the flow steps, the pile-ups, their two-lists note if they made one (the no-sign-off list is where the first wins live), plus answering business questions without them digging, and writing (quotes, emails, posts). *(Follow up: who else will use the Brain, just you, you plus a couple, the whole team eventually?)*
38. **What do you or the team write over and over?** A) Quotes/proposals · B) The same customer emails · C) Invoices and reminders · D) Social posts/marketing · E) Reports · F) Job handovers or briefs · G) A few of these.
39. **Is there anything Claude should never touch or do without asking?** A) Money things: invoices, pricing · B) Anything customer-facing before I've seen it · C) Certain files or folders (tell me which) · D) Just the standard hard rules are fine.
40. **Three months from now, what would make you say this Brain was worth it?** A) Hours back every week · B) Team finds answers without asking me · C) Nothing falls through the cracks · D) The business is less dependent on my memory · E) All of the above, honestly.

**The coverage check: run it before you propose anything.** Step 5 lists every page you are about to write. Go down that list: for each page, could you write it right now, from what you've learned? A page you couldn't write means a question you haven't asked. Ask it before moving on. Never propose a structure you can only half-fill.

## Step 3: propose, then wait

Propose what goes *inside* the core. The top-level folders never change, so what you're proposing is:

- **The subfolders**, named in their words. The departments from question 20 are your strongest guide (their `70-Operations/` might hold `jobs/` and `sops/`; a retailer's `50-Products-Sales/` might hold `ranges/`). Only propose subfolders their answers actually call for. An empty core folder is fine; it fills when the business gets there.
- **The starter pages** you'll write and where each lives, the pages listed in Step 5, populated by the loops: one per offer, per key customer, per person, per important vendor.
- **Where each of their existing documents will be filed**, one line each.

Show it as one tree with a one-line purpose per item. Then ask permission to build it. **Do not create, move, or change anything until they say yes.** If they want changes, adjust and show it again.

## Step 4: build it all

On the yes, do the whole job:

- Create every approved subfolder.
- File every existing document into the folder that owns it. Anything you're not sure about stays in `05-Inbox/`. Never guess a file into a folder. Write `05-Inbox/_needs-sorting.md` listing each flagged file and why you weren't sure.
- Every page and document you file or write gets a `[[wikilink]]` line in its folder's `MOC-*.md`. Bulk material, dozens of same-kind raw files, gets one unlinked line naming the set and its count.
- The voice emails from the shoebox, the ones that sound how they want to sound, are voice samples, not records. File them together in `20-Brand-Marketing/voice-samples/`, one file each.
- Never delete anything. Filing means moving, not removing. A shoebox subfolder left empty once its files are filed may be removed. That is tidying, not deleting.

## Step 5: write it down

The interview's answers land in specific places. This mapping is the whole point. It is also the coverage check's checklist:

- **`CLAUDE.md`**: the lean constitution. Fill the **Who we are** identity block (name, one-line what/who, locations and countries, website and profiles, the departments in their words, the team in one line, how the business sounds) and every other ADJUST-ME block, add anything from question 39 to the hard rules, keep the four standard hard rules exactly as they are, and remove the ADJUST-ME markers. Read the result back in two or three lines.
- **`10-Business/business-model.md`** covers how the business operates today, organised: what it sells and to whom, where it operates and which countries it serves (and how delivery differs), revenue shape, the end-to-end flow of a job, the week's rhythm, the repeat cycles, how money comes in, what's written down and what lives in heads, where information and decisions live, then, briefly, what they expect with Claude in place. This is the page a new GM would want on day one.
- **`10-Business/people/`** gets one page per person (Part 5): role, department, what they own, what they can decide without the owner. Outside help (accountant, VA, agency) gets one page each too.
- **`10-Business/vendors/`** gets one page per important vendor from the vendor loop: what they supply, why them, how critical (the vanished-tomorrow answer), and any terms mentioned.
- **`10-Business/competitors.md`**: who the customers compare them to, what each competitor does well, and why customers pick this business instead, in the owner's own words.
- **`10-Business/tools-and-systems.md`** gets one line per tool from the tools loop: what it runs, who uses it. Where customer data lives (question 29) goes here too.
- **`10-Business/locations/`** applies only if question 2 named more than one site: one page per location, saying what happens there and who runs it. A single base is a line in `business-model.md`, not a folder.
- **`20-Brand-Marketing/voice.md`**: how the business should sound (question 11), in a few plain rules. If voice samples were filed, the page points at `20-Brand-Marketing/voice-samples/` and CLAUDE.md gets one line: "How we sound: anything written for outside eyes matches `20-Brand-Marketing/voice.md`."
- **`30-Customers/customers.md`**: who buys, in their words (questions 5 and 16), plus **one page per named key customer** from question 17 or the shoebox (what we do for them, terms, who the contact is, when the agreement ends), linking its proposals, agreements and invoices.
- **`20-Brand-Marketing/online-presence.md`**: the website and every profile from question 8, one line each on what it's for, plus how new work arrives (question 10) and anything the read-through learned about how the business presents itself.
- **`50-Products-Sales/`** gets **one page per offer from the offer loop**, in the example-page shape: what it is, the price, how it's delivered, who buys it. The offer loop, not the revenue question, is the source. Small and occasional offers get pages too.
- **`50-Products-Sales/pipeline.md`** covers enquiry to customer (questions 18 to 19): the steps, who does them, how long they take, and what happens to the ones that don't say yes.
- **`70-Operations/sops/`** gets one page per how-we-do-things document the shoebox held, linking the original file; and **`70-Operations/meetings/`** (or the owning folder's `meetings/`) gets one page per transcript on file, in the filed-transcript format from `90-Skills/transcript-filing.md`.
- **`80-Finance/money-flow.md`** covers how the money comes in (question 30): invoicing rhythm, payment terms, who raises and who chases. The figures themselves go to the ledger.
- **`00-Meta/numbers-ledger.md`**: every real number the interview, the website, or the shoebox produced (prices, revenue shape, team size, payment terms), each with its source and as-of date. No number anywhere else.
- **Each folder's `MOC-*.md`** lists every page and document the folder owns as a `[[wikilink]]`; bulk material as one unlinked line per set. The MOCs are the Brain's hubs: that is where its shape comes from.
- **`00-Meta/index.md`**: a row for each folder's MOC plus the pages that matter at business level, the Where column as a `[[wikilink]]`. Not every page, because the MOCs already list those.
- **`00-Meta/team-rules.md`** names folder owners: the people Part 5 named for each department; the owner everywhere else. Say so in the read-back.
- Every page you write follows `10-Business/_example-page.md`: Sources wikilink the one or two documents it was actually built from, Related at most one earned link. Most pages carry two links or fewer; the MOC is what connects them.
- **`00-Meta/log.md`** gets the first entry: the date, "Structure Interview run", and one line on what was built.

## Step 6: end with the next step

Finish by telling them exactly one thing: **open this folder in Obsidian** (Open folder as vault). Nothing else. No optional extras, no "you could also". One clear next step.
