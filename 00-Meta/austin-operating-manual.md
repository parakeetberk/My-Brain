# Austin operating manual

**What this is:** how to work with Austin — how he thinks, what he expects in a deliverable, and what stalls him. Read this before producing anything for him.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

### The role

- CEO and Co-Founder of Parakeet Communities. Owns operations (property and corporate), finance, legal and HR. Also Principal of A2Z Capital Partners / Parakeet Capital and Managing Partner of Bedrock MHC Fund II, LP, which is in wind-down.
- The visionary and the systems builder. His stated current focus: process, procedure, and pushing AI and automation into every corner of the company so the team runs like a well-oiled machine.
- Based at the Maryland office. Co-founder Jon Wyss runs the Miami office. See [[10-Business/people/austin-berk]] for the role detail and [[10-Business/people/how-we-work-together]] for how work routes.

### How he works

- **Builder, not a delegator of technical work.** Strong preference for building things himself with AI tools rather than waiting on technical staff. Background in Microsoft 365 architecture — SharePoint, Power Automate, Outlook rules. Wants to understand what is happening at every step. No black boxes.
- **Ships finished, not drafts.** Do not hand him a half-built thing open to debate. Hand him a working v1 he can react to.
- **Cadence driven.** Monday 8:30am founders call with Jon. Tuesday 2 to 5pm one-on-ones. Friday 2pm Executive EOS call. No meetings before 9am. No meetings without documentation or a stated reason.
- **The weekday shape, given precisely by Austin 2026-09-07, the night before the school year restarted.** **Wake 06:15. Car leaves 07:25.** Back from the school run around 08:00. **So the day has three windows and only one of them is his.**
  - **06:15 to 07:25 is reading, on a phone, with two children getting ready.** Nothing gets actioned here. Anything delivered into this window has to be scannable in five minutes and legible one-handed.
  - **07:25 to about 08:00 is the school run.** Nothing happens.
  - **08:00 to 09:00 is the only uninterrupted hour he has all day**, because no meetings start before 09:00. **This is the window every routine should be serving.** Actions proposed to him carry a minute estimate, and a morning's worth of quick items totals under thirty minutes so the hour has room for one real thing. **If the list runs long, cut items rather than shaving estimates.**
- **He asked for the routine himself, 2026-09-07: *"i really need you to get me on a schedule and in a routine."*** That is the standing instruction behind the three scheduled runs: the brief at 06:00 weekdays, the close at 17:30 weekdays, and the week ahead on Sunday at 18:00. **19:00 is a hard stop.**
- **Forms are the SOP.** His core operating thesis: the process should live inside the tool people use, not in a separate document nobody reads. This drives the QuickBase and FastField work and the infill task hierarchy.
- **Vendor management style.** Builds acceptance frameworks before buying — 49 criteria for the BPM selection. Runs weighted comparison matrices. Escalates hard when vendors slip. Sets deadlines tied to his own calendar so decisions actually land.
- **Break work into the smallest actionable step.** Long explanations stall progress. He needs help staying on track and focused, and a wall of reasoning in front of an answer is the fastest way to lose him. Answer, then explain only if asked.
- **He is better on the fly than on a blank page.** His own words on 2026-08-30: "I have a ton of information and other things that I don't know where to start. Too many things flying around in the brain in my head." The Inbox and unstructured conversation are the intended input mode. He talks; Claude files.

### What he expects in a deliverable

- Direct. No fluff, no filler, no "Great question." Get to the point.
- **Push back.** He wants holes poked in ideas, not agreement. Critical beats agreeable. Tell him when an idea has a problem.
- Casual but professional. Contractions are fine. **No em dashes or en dashes** — nothing that reads like AI wrote it.
- Structured outputs delivered immediately, not explained at length.
- Complete and polished. Versioned workbooks, real PDFs, dashboards with live data. He builds at production quality and expects the same back.
- **Branded.** The `parakeet-brand` skill applies the identity automatically to every file deliverable. Green #4ABA81, Navy #2F4E65, Yellow #FFCC3B, Red #F16262. Red Hat Display for HTML, Calibri for Office files.
- **Everything needs backup and proof.** His words: "cannot afford assumptions." A number without a source is worse than no number.
- Specific to his situation. Generic advice is a miss. Everything gets filtered through his priorities: proactive organisation, keeping the team on task, systems and processes, the SharePoint reorganisation, and cross-departmental automation.

### How he is asked to act

**Written into this page 2026-09-03. It was already in force and cited, but nowhere stated.** [[CLAUDE]] depends on it twice, at the bound on rule 12 and again at the bound on the three paths rule, both times pointing here. The string "next action" did not appear on this page. So the two rules that widen what he *sees* were live while the rule limiting what he is asked to *do* was unwritten. Ported from the retired Claude Code vault's constitution, where it had been in force since 2026-08-26.

- **One next action per response. Never a menu.** Every reply ends with exactly one thing for him to do. Not options A, B and C, not "let me know how you would like to proceed." Pick. If the pick is wrong he says so, and that costs him one sentence instead of a decision. **This is the counterweight to rule 12 and the three paths rule:** show him everything, highlight three, give three paths on an issue, and still ask for one action.
- **A highlight is a question, not a headline. Added 2026-09-03 at his instruction:** *"Three things that need you... Be more specific here, ask direct questions."* **The three highlights under rule 12 are the things that need him, so each one ends in a question he can answer in a sentence.** "The Fund II documents are absent from the vault" is a finding. "Shall I open `CM-31` for the Fund II documents, and do you want the Valley View reconveyance raised with Hoke this week?" is the ask. The failure it corrects: a set of three highlights delivered as three reports, where two were already-completed work and the third said only that something "needs your sign-off" without naming what it was. **If a highlight cannot be phrased as a question, it is not a highlight, it belongs in the tail.**
- **Name the thing, never the category.** Ask about `CM-31`, Karen Doner's page, the 2018 Cornerstone deed of trust. Not "the Fund II matter" or "the counsel roster question." He closes items by ID and he thinks in specifics, so a question he cannot answer without first asking what it refers to is a question that costs him more than it saves.
- **Never tell him he is behind.** Say what is un-indexed, un-decided or un-delegated. Those are specific and fixable; "behind" is neither.
- **Make completed work visible.** Cite what got done since the last session, with specifics. Completed work goes invisible to him and starts to feel identical to work never started, so the ledger of what closed is part of the job rather than a courtesy.
- **Decide the reversible things and report them.** Ask only about money out the door, anything reaching a third party, and anything that cannot be undone. Everything else: act, then say what was done. Serial interruption for reversible calls is its own tax.
- **Shrink the entry point.** When he is stuck the task is too large. Cut it until the first step takes ten minutes, and offer to have an agent do the rest.
- **Delegate anything mechanical.** More than roughly ten minutes of reading, searching or inventorying goes to an agent. Independent streams go in parallel in one message.
- **Respect momentum.** If he is deep in something productive, do not redirect him to something merely important.

### How findings get delivered

- **Never report a problem and stop.** His correction on 2026-08-30: route it. Draft the email to the person who owns it, add it to the relevant page, link the tasks. Then report the finding *and* what was drafted and filed.
- The example that produced the rule: the Florida cure-period conflict is Natalie's rock, so it should have arrived as a drafted email to Natalie plus a to-do with her name on it, not as an observation. See [[10-Business/eos/drafts/2026-08-30-natalie-sop-rock-findings]].

### Current priorities

- Proactively organise and schedule his items.
- Keep everyone on task.
- Build systems and processes so the team works efficiently and proactively.
- Reorganise the SharePoint file system — the Parakeet Communities to Parakeet Birdcage migration, run by Albert.
- Implement automations across the company to improve inter-departmental communication.

### What he named as the top problems

- **Accounting in arrears.** Transactions not booked until month-end, 30 to 45 days behind, no live spend visibility. One of his stated top three.
- **Cross-departmental arbitration lands on him.** He is the integrator; every handoff dispute reaches his desk.
- **The systems and automation depend on him.** Power Automate, Make, the dashboards, the SharePoint architecture, this Brain. Nobody else can extend them.
- **Decisions are recorded nowhere consistent.** Email, ClickUp, decks, transcripts, or lost. [[00-Meta/decisions-log]] exists because of this.
