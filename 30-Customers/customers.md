# Customers

**What this is:** Parakeet has two customer bases — the residents who rent lots and buy homes, and the capital partners who fund the platform. This page is the taxonomy, in the company's own words.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

### Two customer bases

- **Residents** buy the product: a lot or a site in a community, and often a home to put on it. Everything in [[50-Products-Sales/the-community]] is sold to them.
- **Capital partners** fund the platform: the fund-level equity, the debt, the advisors and the legacy LP base. They sit in `30-Customers/capital-partners/` and they are customers in the sense that matters — the company reports to them, is measured by them, and raises from them.

### The resident-side taxonomy, in their own words

- **"Resident" is the default word, not tenant.** It is used for homeowners and renters alike, and it is what the Employee Handbook uses when it defines the flock as "residents, employees, vendors, and stakeholders."
- **"Tenant" is reserved for RV and long-term rental context.** Rent Manager's own module is called Tenants, so the word survives in the system even where the company would say resident.
- **The load-bearing distinction is homeowner versus renter.** A **homeowner** owns the home and rents the lot — the company's preferred customer, because the home and its overhead belong to them. A **renter** rents a company-owned home, which the company is deliberately reducing ([[50-Products-Sales/poh-rentals]]). The whole 2026 conversion programme is described as "convert renters to owners."
- **"Landlord" means a third party who owns homes inside a community and rents them out** — a distinct and problematic stakeholder class, not a customer in the ordinary sense. At Regency I on 2026-08-19: "I know we have over 10 landlords, but I think there's at least close to 20-30 landlords." One named landlord holds close to 20 units at rents as high as $2,000. The engaged ones run well-kept lots; the absentee ones do not, and the community carries the consequence.
- **"Prospect" is a lead; "buyer" is a purchaser.** Rent Manager's Prospect statuses are the formal set: New Prospect, Application Completed, Approved, Denied, Lost. The funnel stage names the team uses are **"new prospect" to "engaged prospect" to "tour completed" to "application completed."**
- **Snowbirds** are the seasonal Florida customer and they set the calendar: August is dead, the ramp is October, and the 55+ communities — Sea Shells and Southgate are the named ones — are where they concentrate.
- **55+ and 50+ residents** are a formal category, not a marketing one: 14 communities are fifty-five-plus and 1 is fifty-plus, against 53 all-ages ([[60-Communities/portfolio]]).

### What they actually care about

- **The total monthly payment**, not the home price. The company prices against a $1,448 two-bedroom market rent less a 10% discount, and knows a financed infill home at full cost lands about 20% above that ([[50-Products-Sales/home-sales]]).
- **Community condition.** Roads, drainage, lighting, mailboxes, signage, a clean clubhouse. The August tour is a catalogue of exactly this, and the internal standard was stated bluntly on 2026-08-22: "our residents shouldn't walk into a clubhouse and see a dead cockroach on the floor."
- **Safety.** The website's own promise is "a safe place to live"; the incident reporting and PPE processes are the internal side of it.
- **A manager who answers.** 28 of 29 on-site manager slots are filled, plus an offshore resident call centre ([[10-Business/people/field-roster]]).
- **Being treated as a neighbour, not a tenant.** Residents are addressed as "the flock" — "Join the Flock", "friendly flock of residents" ([[20-Brand-Marketing/online-presence]]).

### How the company qualifies them

- **Screening standard: gross monthly income at or above 2.5× the monthly lot rent.** Below that is a basis for denial, and the calculation is documented in Rent Manager.
- **Screening runs through AmRent inside Rent Manager, never outside either system**, takes up to 72 hours, and no preliminary decision may be communicated before full results are received ([[10-Business/vendors/amrent]]).
- **A denial notice never goes to the prospect before the Community Manager is notified**, and must comply with fair housing and adverse-action notice requirements.
- **The full path from enquiry to resident, with owners and clocks, is on [[50-Products-Sales/pipeline]].**

### Known problems on the resident side

- **Applicants abandon applications**, often over concerns about their own history, and the counter-play is to call them before they pay the non-refundable fee.
- **Legacy renters repeatedly fail credit approval on conversion** — "denial after denial" — which is what the AutoMHatic lower-credit buckets and the LOP exist to absorb.
- **Two generations of SOP 006 disagree on who approves a borderline applicant** — the Directors of Sales and Operations jointly, or the Regional Manager. Unresolved.
- **The two documents also disagree on the terminal state of a denial** — status Denied, or moved to Lost — which changes reporting.
- **Landlords have no policy behind them.** Nothing in the vault sets a position on third-party landlords inside a community: no cap, no registration, no standard, no enforcement path.
