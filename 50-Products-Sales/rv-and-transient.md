# RV and transient

**What this is:** 950 RV sites across the portfolio, sold by the night, week, month or season — the most seasonal, highest-rent and least-documented part of the product set.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

- **950 RV sites across the 67 active communities**, with **18 communities carrying a nonzero 2026 RV rent** (Property Data Sheet, as of 2026-08-30, via [[60-Communities/portfolio]]).
- **2026 RV site rent runs $600 to $2,275 a month and averages $897** across those 18 (simple average of the 2026 gross figures). Geiger Key RV at $2,275 is the highest rent in the entire portfolio, MH or RV.
- **The largest RV assets:** Great Oak RV 198 sites, Paradise Lakes 175, Vantage Oaks 174, River Oaks RV 110, Whispering Pines FL 64, Triple S Marina 56, Geiger Key RV 36, Belle Haven 34, Silver Breeze 30, Windward Knoll 24.
- **Florida is a snowbird market and the season drives the year.** From 2026-08-24: "nobody's buying in August, right? I mean Florida's a snowbird market… I really expect the influx of demand to come. Let's just say starting in October."
- **RV occupancy was the single largest revenue miss of 2025.** Lighter RV occupancy against budget accounted for roughly **60% of a $920k rental income miss in JREFI**, concentrated at **River Oaks, Vantage Oaks, Lucky Clover and Paradise Lakes** (board deck).
- **The booking system is Campspot** — reservations, site blocking, seasonal pricing and analytics. Rent Manager still holds the assets and the ledger; a River Oaks conversation records "there's still assets in Rent Manager… still sell it through Rent Manager" alongside Campspot for the site bookings. See [[10-Business/vendors/campspot]].
- **The 2026 RV goal, verbatim from the board deck:** "in-fill RV sites with park models and generate incremental revenues from Campspot." Campspot is treated as a revenue line, not just a booking tool.
- **Park-model conversion is the stated direction — "convert rolling stock to park models."** The 2026 JREFI infill plan is **104 park model RV sites against 85 MH lots.** At Vantage Oaks the plan was to set two NCS models and two park models as soon as permits arrived. On 2026-08-24: RV visitors "coming down that can be sold into. Pretty amazing fifty thousand dollars park models that they can seasonally come down to."
- **A six-month minimum stay was decided in August on the full-season sites**, reducible to three months if bookings are slow, with the smaller west-side sites kept open for short-term traffic and the season sites blocked in Campspot.
- **The problem that decision fixes.** At the Florida Whispering Pines on 2026-08-20: short-term bookings on Campspot were tying up lots meant for long stays, **small units were landing on the large 50-amp sites**, and the system would not let a long-term resident be booked past 2026.
- **Marketing for RV was a Q1 rock and its status is unconfirmed:** "Get up the standalone RV websites and Parakeet Retreats page for RV site marketing." **No evidence in the vault that either launched**, and the March deck still lists the standalone RV website and Parakeet Retreats page "status and launch timeline" as an open question. See [[20-Brand-Marketing/online-presence]].
- **RV marketing in August was ad hoc:** a block-and-blast Facebook campaign and a single test RV listing at Belle Haven; RV park canvassing to find where snowbird traffic goes, plus flyers, at River Oaks.
- **The deck's own RV worry:** "Triple S: 12 vacant RV sites heading into season – need marketing strategy. What's the immediate plan to fill them?"
- **The vocabulary matters here:** an RV rentable unit is a **site**, never a lot and never a pad. "Tenant" rather than "resident" is the word the company reserves for RV and long-term rental context ([[30-Customers/customers]]).
- **Ancillary revenue rides on the RV assets:** the boat slips and lifts at Triple S Marina and the Geiger Key Fish Camp restaurant, both on [[50-Products-Sales/ancillary-income]].

### The compliance gap, and it belongs on this page

- **RV parks fall under Florida Chapter 513, not Chapter 723, and no prospectus applies.** SOPs 801, 802 and 804 say so and explicitly exclude or route RV parks: "RV parks are Chapter 513 — no prospectus applies; route to Legal."
- **No Chapter 513 / RV compliance SOP exists anywhere in the library, so Legal has nothing to route to.** Rent increases, notices, violations, evictions and deposits at Geiger Key, Great Oak and River Oaks have **no documented path**. Critical on [[00-Meta/open-items]].
- **RV appears in the operating SOPs only as a $500 checkbox on the eviction intake form.** There is no RV collections path in SOP 100, no booking, cancellation, seasonal-rate or site-blocking procedure anywhere, and no statement of who owns the Campspot account.
- **Three of the seven communities with no prospectus on file are RV parks:** Geiger Key RV, Great Oak RV and River Oaks RV. All three must stop and route to Legal on any rent increase. See [[10-Business/departments/legal-and-compliance]].
- **Four of the five unreliable December-2025 occupancy rows are RV-heavy properties** — Great Oak RV, Paradise Lakes, River Oaks RV — so the RV half of the portfolio is also the least reliably measured.

## Decisions

- 2026-08-20 — Set a six-month minimum stay on the sites historically used for full-season rentals, reducible to three months if bookings are slow, keeping the smaller west side sites short-term, and block the season sites in Campspot. (Source: [[70-Operations/meetings/2026-08-southeast-tour/2026-08-20-whispering-pines-mh-and-rv-site-visit]])
- 2026-08-21 — Set two NCS models and two park models at Vantage Oaks as soon as permits arrive, even with electrical hookups pending, using a staged viewing protocol. (Source: [[70-Operations/meetings/2026-08-southeast-tour/2026-08-21-vantage-oaks-site-visit]])
