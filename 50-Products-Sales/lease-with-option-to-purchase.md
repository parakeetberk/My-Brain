# Lease with option to purchase

**What this is:** the LOP — a lease on a company-owned home with a contractual purchase option at end of term. The bridge for a buyer a lender has declined, and the route by which a renter becomes an owner.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

- **The field term is "LOP."** The March deck also calls the same shape "RTO" (rent to own), and the board deck counts legacy RTOs separately from POHs — three names for one economic structure, which is worth knowing when reading any count.
- **The governing process is [[70-Operations/sops/003a-lease-with-option-to-purchase]].** It is one of the three deal paths a signed offer routes to, alongside Cash and Financed ([[50-Products-Sales/home-sales]]).
- **When it is offered: ideally when a buyer pursuing a financed purchase is declined by the lender**, or on another qualifying financial obstacle — change of employment, thin credit history, credit repair, divorce proceedings. **Explicitly not a standard first offer.**
- **Three commercial terms need Director of Sales approval before the offer is submitted: the LOP end-of-term purchase price, the monthly home payment, and the term of the agreement.**
- **The lease-structure rule that matters: the Community Lease must reflect the lot rent portion only**, not the combined home-and-lot payment. The total monthly lot and home rent is accounted for in the LOP document itself.
- **The date rule that matters: the Community Lease must expire the day before lot rent increases go into effect.** The lease end date is confirmed against the rent increase schedule before issuing.
- **The clocks.** 24 hours for Director of Sales approval; 24 hours for buyer e-sign via SignEasy or wet signature. **All deadlines anchor to the lease start date stated in the LOP, and that date does not move.**
- **The package:** LOP Agreement, Community Lease, Rules and Regulations, and the Prospectus in Florida only.
- **How it closes out.** The Move-In Request Form goes in the same day as the lease start date, with the Closing Coordinator copied. Asset Management moves Prospect to Resident and marks the asset inactive once LOP funds are confirmed and documented. Sales adjusts the Rent Manager Asset UDFs to modify the listing feed and updates the CRM; Operations uploads the executed documents to the Rent Manager Prospect record.
- **A recorded example, from the 2026 infill plan at Belle Haven, verbatim:** "When the rehabs are ready, program for existing resident sales/LOPs at 750 lot rent + 550 LOP payment monthly for 3 years. Also list for sale first so it shows we are giving existing residents a deal." So $750 lot rent plus a $550 monthly home payment over three years. See [[60-Communities/belle-haven]].
- **A second recorded example, 2026-08-18:** one approval came through and "we did a LOP for three years, and it's going to pay off the home." A buyer with a truck loan that would take three years to pay off was put on an LOP once, on Leslie Croft's call, because the house was cheap enough.
- **Legacy LOPs were inherited with acquisitions and are burning off.** At The Oaks, "legacy lease-option (LOP) agreements from a prior purchase continue, about six of which have paid off." Across JREFII the board deck counts **100 RTOs at March 2026 against 405 POHs, with 5 RTOs burned off since acquisition.**
- **The strategic use, from 2026-08-22 at The Oaks:** no new incoming renters; renew existing renters and keep converting renters to owners, **with buyout options considered to accelerate LOP conversions.**
- **Who runs it:** the Community Manager or Regional Sales Rep prepares and submits; the Director of Sales approves ([[10-Business/people/leslie-croft]]); the Closing Coordinator compiles ([[10-Business/people/melanie-sanchez-soto]]); Asset Management closes the loop ([[10-Business/people/justin-minkoff]]).
- **The lender-side alternative it competes with:** the AutoMHatic renter conversion programme, launched March 2026, filters prospects into credit buckets and approves them for consumer finance instead — 500 to 580 credit score at 10% to 12% over 20 years, above 580 at roughly 8% over 20 years. See [[50-Products-Sales/poh-rentals]].

### Named problems

- **The SOP's own status field reads "Approved" while the tracker, the wiki index and the wiki folder all mark it a draft.**
- **The rejected-LOP response window is 2 days in 003a and 3 days in the closing SOP**, and the closing checklist leaves the number blank.
- **A lender-coordination phase is pasted into an SOP whose whole purpose is the absence of a lender.** 003a's Phase 4 table reads "Buyer initiates and maintains communication with lender… follows up every 48 hours… once lender issues clear to close" — copied verbatim from the financed closing SOP. The phase list at the top only has four phases and does not include it.
- **No end-of-term exercise procedure exists anywhere.** The SOP covers signing the LOP; nothing covers what happens when the option is exercised, expires or is declined — which is the whole point of the instrument.
- **Georgia signs electronically here** while the combined closing document states globally that "South Carolina and Georgia must wet sign all documents."
- **The LOP Checklist is listed as "Linked. Compiled in SignEasy" and is not present in the library as a file.** Formal onboarding training for the process is [TBD].
- **The March deck records RTO structure as undefined** — "RTO structure and price drop triggers" both sit on its open-questions list.
