# Ancillary income

**What this is:** the revenue lines that are not lot rent and not a home sale — boat slips, a restaurant, utility reimbursement, and fees. Small next to rent, but two of them are real operating businesses.

**Status:** current — 2026-08-30

## The facts

### Boat slips and lifts — Triple S Marina

- **Triple S Marina in Atlantic Beach, NC is a 234-unit community with a marina attached** — 123 MH lots, 56 RV sites and 55 other units. See [[60-Communities/triple-s-marina]].
- **A second tranche of 19 boat lifts was approved for July 2026 at $252,000**, following what the board deck calls "the strong performance of the initial boat lift installations."
- **The economics, verbatim from the board deck:** "At an average cost of approximately $14,000 per slip, rents increase from $215 to $625 per month, generating an estimated 35% return on investment."
- **A third tranche is already budgeted: $266,000 of 2027 Boat Slip to Lift Conversions**, high priority, owned by Construction, scheduled 2027-01-31 to 2027-06-30 (CapEx Overview workbook).
- Triple S also carries the **highest MH lot rent in the portfolio at $1,367** and runs a payroll deficit of $3,270 against the 5%-of-revenue allocation.

### The Geiger Key Fish Camp restaurant

- **Geiger Key is two entities, not one.** The board deck's JREFI yield table lists **Geiger Key Resort Propco at $13,688,238** and **Geiger Key Fish Camp Opco at $2,761,762**, both acquired 8/31/2022. The restaurant is an OpCo with its own entity stack.
- **What it is:** "a one-of-a-kind hybrid RV resort with on-site marina and waterfront restaurant minutes from Key West," acquired August 2022 for $14.8MM with an adjacent expansion parcel acquired December 2023 for $1.65MM.
- **It missed income by $275k in 2025.** The board deck's commentary, verbatim: "GKFC missed income by $275k. The restaurant's GM departed at the start of Q3. Performance in 1H was negatively impacted as she was checked out during her final months. She has been replaced and the new manager has transitioned into the role effectively."
- **The 2026 budget assumes recovery from the new GM:** "GKFC revenue from improved menu pricing and other initiatives from new GM," with "New GM driving significant improvements – menu prices, payroll efficiency, & event programming."
- **The restaurant distorts the portfolio's reported margins**, which is why the board deck reports NOI margin twice — with and without the Geiger OpCo. For JREFI 2025: 57% reported, **66% excluding the Geiger OpCo.**
- **Restaurant income and expense are separately stated in the debt appendix: $3,592,791 of restaurant income against $1,407,272 of restaurant expense.**
- **It is a named reason to sell the asset.** The disposition rationale includes "Re-invest into in-fill and growth initiatives and **simplifies portfolio (restaurant)**", alongside Geiger being the highest-storm-exposure asset in JREFI. See [[60-Communities/geiger-key-rv-resort]].

### Utility reimbursement income

- **The mechanic that matters: residents are billed pro rata on a total lot basis, regardless of whether a lot is vacant or occupied**, so reimbursement income rises with occupancy (board deck, JREFI 2026 budget commentary, verbatim).
- **The board deck states the consequence directly:** "Utility Expense growth can be entirely attributed to higher occupancy, which is recovered in Reimbursement Income."
- **The numbers in JREFI:** reimbursement income $1,189,834 in 2025 actual, budgeted **$1,415,798 for 2026, +19%.** In JREFII: $1,038,505 in 2025 actual against $801,813 budgeted for 2026, −23%.
- **A 2025 distortion worth knowing:** switching billing vendors moved terms from net 60 to net 30, so in several cases **13 months of utilities were paid in fiscal 2025.**
- **Utility recapture is where the process gap is worst. Four of the seven Tier 1 Critical processes with nothing started are utility processes** — utility onboarding for current communities, utility onboarding at acquisitions, utility recapture reporting and utility recapture review. The only documented utility process is activation, [[70-Operations/sops/303-utility-activation]], and its request form has three different owners across three SOPs.
- **Utility optimisation is a 2026 goal in two departments at once:** "UTILITY PROCESS REVIEW & IMPROVEMENTS" in Accounting & Finance and "UTILITY OPTIMIZATION – Analytics in tandem with Acctg team" in Asset Management. **Utilities and operating permits were decided in August to move out of accounting.** See [[10-Business/departments/accounting-and-finance]].

### Fee and miscellaneous income

- **JREFI fee and misc income: $4,102,304 in 2025 actual against $4,374,052 budgeted, a 6% miss** — the Geiger restaurant sits inside this line, which is why the $275k GKFC shortfall shows up here.
- **JREFII fee and misc income: $248,718 in 2025 actual, budgeted $414,428 for 2026, +67%.**
- **Fees the field actually charges** sit in the resident lifecycle SOPs: the non-refundable application fee ([[70-Operations/sops/006-lead-intake-tracking-application-and-screening]]), late fees and attorney fees posted to a resident account before any eviction-stopping payment is accepted ([[70-Operations/sops/107-eviction]]), and violation fines, which at Regency I were credited with moving residents to clean up their lots.
- **Nothing in the vault records a fee schedule by community**, and no SOP covers fee setting or fee review.
